Title: Athena Author Backs Amodei's Call to Pace Frontier AI, Warns of Widening Readiness Gap
Content:
<p>The author behind the Athena project has publicly endorsed Anthropic chief executive Dario Amodei's argument for slowing frontier AI development, in an update published shortly after Amodei's own essay and carried by The Hacker News on 6 November.</p>
<p>The post opens with Amodei's essay <em>We Must Pace the Frontier</em>, published on Saturday, and sends readers straight to it. "I agree with him. Go read it if you haven't," the author writes — then goes on to lay out a set of forward-looking predictions that reflect the author's own outlook rather than established findings.</p>
<p>At the centre of the piece is a practitioner's account of watching capability outrun readiness. "I've spent the summer looking at what these models find, and the gap he describes between what they can do and what the rest of us are ready for is the one I've been staring at since May," the author writes. It is an experiential observation, not a formal study — but it is the same gap Amodei flags, arrived at independently over months of hands-on work.</p>
<p>The post's title, <em>The Flood is Coming And The Pipes Were Already Full</em>, distils the metaphor: volume arriving faster than the systems meant to carry it can absorb. That framing positions the update squarely alongside Amodei's case for deliberate pacing of model releases, at a moment when the speed of frontier deployment remains contested across the policy debate.</p>
<p>In the excerpt carried by The Hacker News, the author turns to what is described as "the hardest piece of" Amodei's argument before the passage breaks off. The original post picks up from there and carries the complete set of predictions — all to be read as the author's personal outlook rather than verified forecasts.</p>
<p>One theme will be familiar to any team deploying AI at scale: capability routinely arrives ahead of the governance, monitoring, and operational capacity needed to use it safely. The author's warning is that this gap has been widening since May — and the full argument for what should be done about it is worth reading at the source.</p>
<hr>
<p>Athena 項目的作者日前公開支持 Anthropic 行政總裁 Dario Amodei 關於放慢前沿 AI 發展的論點，該更新在 Amodei 本人文章發表後不久發布，並於 11 月 6 日刊載於 The Hacker News。</p>
<p>文章開篇即提及 Amodei 於星期六發表的《We Must Pace the Frontier》，並直接引導讀者前往閱讀。「我同意他的觀點。如果你還沒看過，去讀一下吧，」作者寫道——隨後提出一組前瞻性預測，反映的是作者本人的看法，而非已獲確立的研究結論。</p>
<p>整篇文章的核心，是一位從業人員對能力發展超越就緒程度的觀察記錄。「整個夏天我都在研究這些模型的發現，他所描述的模型能力與其他人準備程度之間的差距，正是我自五月以來一直注視的那個差距，」作者寫道。這是經驗性的觀察，並非正式研究——但它與 Amodei 所指出的是同一個差距，是透過數月實踐工作獨立得出的結論。</p>
<p>文章標題《The Flood is Coming And The Pipes Were Already Full》精煉了這個比喻：湧入的速度，超過了本應承載它的系統所能吸納的速度。這種框架使該更新與 Amodei 主張審慎控制模型發布節奏的論述互相呼應，而目前前沿部署速度的快慢在政策討論中仍具爭議。</p>
<p>在 The Hacker News 刊載的節錄中，作者在段落中斷之前，談及被形容為 Amodei 論點中「最困難的一部分」。原始文章從該處繼續，載有完整的預測集合——全部應理解為作者本人的看法，而非經核實的預測。</p>
<p>對於任何大規模部署 AI 的團隊而言，其中一個主題並不陌生：能力往往先於安全運用它所需的治理、監控及營運能力而到來。作者的警告是，這個差距自五月以來一直在擴大——而關於應如何應對的完整論述，值得到原文閱讀。</p>
