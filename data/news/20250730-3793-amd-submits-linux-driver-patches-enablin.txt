Title: AMD Submits Linux Driver Patches Enabling HDMI 2.1 ALLM and Variable Refresh Rate
Content:
<p>AMD has posted new patches to the AMDGPU kernel driver to implement two more features of the HDMI 2.1 specification: Auto Low-Latency Mode (ALLM) and Variable Refresh Rate (VRR). This step, reported by Phoronix, advances the company's ongoing effort to develop a full HDMI 2.1 implementation in its open-source Linux graphics driver.</p>
<p>The latest submission follows earlier patches that introduced foundational HDMI 2.1 technologies like Fixed Rate Link (FRL) for high-speed signaling and Display Stream Compression (DSC). ALLM enables a display, such as a gaming monitor or TV, to automatically switch to a low-latency mode when it receives a signal from an interactive source like a console or PC. This eliminates the need for users to manually change picture modes. Complementing this, HDMI VRR allows the GPU's frame output to sync dynamically with the display's refresh rate, significantly reducing visual tearing and stutter in games and other dynamic content.</p>
<p>For years, the open-source enablement of HDMI 2.1 on AMD hardware was hampered by licensing constraints and limited public documentation from the HDMI Forum. AMD's sequence of upstream patches—first FRL, then DSC, and now ALLM and VRR—demonstrates a deliberate strategy to incrementally unlock these features. This methodical approach is clearing past barriers to bring the open-source AMDGPU driver's capabilities closer to parity with the company's proprietary Windows driver.</p>
<p>When these patches are eventually merged into the mainline Linux kernel and adopted by distributions, Linux users with AMD Radeon GPUs will benefit directly. Gamers and content creators connecting high-end HDMI 2.1 displays should experience smoother, more responsive video without requiring workarounds or closed-source components. This progress also strengthens the broader open-source ecosystem by narrowing the feature gap between free and proprietary graphics stacks, making AMD hardware a more compelling choice for dedicated Linux environments.</p>
<p>It is important to note, as Phoronix indicates, that the patches are still under review. They require extensive testing across various GPU generations and display devices to ensure stability and compatibility before they can be considered production-ready. Nevertheless, the steady cadence of HDMI 2.1 feature updates from AMD's Linux team signals sustained commitment to modern open-source graphics support.</p>

<p>AMD已向AMDGPU核心驅動程式提交新補丁，以實現HDMI 2.1規範中的兩項額外功能：自動低延遲模式（ALLM）與可變刷新率（VRR）。據Phoronix報道，此舉推進了該公司在其開源Linux圖形驅動程式中開發完整HDMI 2.1實現方案的持續努力。</p>
<p>此次最新提交延續了早前奠定基礎的HDMI 2.1技術補丁，包括用於高速信號傳輸的固定速率鏈路（FRL）以及顯示串流壓縮（DSC）。ALLM使顯示器（例如遊戲顯示器或電視）能在接收來自遊戲主機或電腦等互動源的信號時，自動切換至低延遲模式，無需用戶手動更改畫面模式。作為補充，HDMI VRR允許GPU的幀輸出與顯示器的刷新率動態同步，顯著減少遊戲及其他動態內容中的畫面撕裂與卡頓現象。</p>
<p>多年來，AMD硬件上HDMI 2.1的開源支援一直受限於HDMI論壇的授權條款與有限的公開文件。AMD此次上游補丁的提交順序——先是FRL，繼而DSC，現如今ALLM與VRR——展現了一項循序漸進解鎖這些功能的明確策略。這種有條不紊的方法正在消除過往障礙，使開源AMDGPU驅動程式的功能逐步逼近該公司專有Windows驅動程式的水平。</p>
<p>當這些補丁最終合併至Linux主線內核並被各發行版採用時，使用AMD Radeon GPU的Linux用戶將直接受益。連接高端HDMI 2.1顯示器的遊戲玩家與內容創作者應能體驗到更流暢、更具響應性的視訊，無需採用變通方案或閉源組件。此進展亦透過縮小自由與專有圖形堆疊之間的功能差距，鞏固了更廣泛的開源生態系統，使AMD硬件成為專業Linux環境中更具吸引力的選擇。</p>
<p>必須指出的是，正如Phoronix所指，相關補丁仍在審查階段。它們需要在各代GPU及顯示設備上進行廣泛測試，以確保穩定性與相容性後方能視為生產就緒。儘管如此，AMD Linux團隊持續推出HDMI 2.1功能更新的穩定節奏，顯示了其對現代開源圖形支援的長期承諾。</p>
