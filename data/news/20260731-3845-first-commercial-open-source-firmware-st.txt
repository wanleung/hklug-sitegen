Title: First Commercial Open-Source Firmware Stack Targets Mainstream AMD Ryzen AM5 Desktops
Content:
<p>```</p>
<p>Firmware specialist 3mdeb has released the first commercially supported open-source firmware package for a mainstream AMD Ryzen AM5 desktop motherboard. As documented by Phoronix, the company has adapted its Dasharo stack—built on Coreboot and AMD's openSIL project—to the widely available MSI PRO B850-P WIFI board.</p>
<p>The release builds on 3mdeb's work last month, when the firm delivered open-source firmware for a Gigabyte AMD EPYC server motherboard. That earlier effort also leveraged AMD's openSIL initiative, which seeks to open-source portions of silicon initialization code. Extending this approach to an AM5 desktop platform shows the Coreboot-plus-openSIL combination can operate on common consumer hardware, not just specialized server boards.</p>
<p>For years, modern x86 systems have resisted fully open firmware. Proprietary UEFI code from motherboard vendors typically remains closed, limiting independent security audits, long-term maintenance, and community-driven fixes. By shipping a functional Dasharo image for the MSI PRO B850-P WIFI, 3mdeb demonstrates that an auditable alternative is technically feasible on current Ryzen desktop silicon.</p>
<p>The practical implications matter for IT and open-source communities. An open firmware stack lets security researchers and administrators inspect initialization code, confirm no unwanted components are present, and apply patches without vendor delays. It also offers a route to extend hardware lifecycles: once a board's official support window closes, an independently maintained firmware could keep receiving updates. 3mdeb positions Dasharo as a commercially supported offering, distinct from volunteer-driven projects, which may bolster reliability and encourage broader adoption.</p>
<p>The MSI PRO B850-P WIFI was chosen as the initial desktop target because it is a current, readily available B850-series board. Support is, for now, limited to this specific model; expanding to other boards will require additional engineering and potential vendor cooperation. Still, a working reference implementation lowers the barrier for other developers and hardware makers exploring openSIL-based solutions.</p>
<p>This release aligns with long-standing goals in the free-software firmware space. Coreboot has long provided a libre foundation for system initialization, but modern AMD platforms have demanded closed binary blobs for certain functions. openSIL reduces that dependency, and 3mdeb's integration work merges the components into a usable product. Organizations and enthusiasts prioritizing supply-chain visibility or maintaining hardware beyond its official end-of-life now have a tangible starting point on AM5.</p>
<p>The project remains early for desktop platforms, yet the commercial backing behind Dasharo distinguishes it from earlier open-firmware efforts that often faltered on sustained maintenance. That support model may prove essential as the stack moves beyond proof-of-concept toward a practical choice for users demanding both openness and accountable upkeep.</p>
<p>The development carries particular relevance for technology professionals evaluating firmware options on new Ryzen-based systems, particularly in regions with active open-source communities. Should support expand to additional boards, an auditable, commercially backed firmware stack could shape purchasing and deployment decisions where transparency and long-term maintainability rank as priorities.</p>
<p>Technical details and download information are available through 3mdeb's Dasharo channels, as covered in the Phoronix report.</p>
