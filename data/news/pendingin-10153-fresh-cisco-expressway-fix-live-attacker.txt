<h3>Fresh Cisco Expressway Fix, Live Attackers: CISA Tells Agencies the RCE Is Being Hit Now</h3>
<p>The US Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) has told federal civilian agencies that attackers are exploiting a recently patched critical remote code execution (RCE) vulnerability in Cisco Expressway in the wild, according to a BleepingComputer report.</p>
<p>The detail that should change how administrators read this advisory is not the severity rating — it is the timeline. A flaw that has only just been fixed is already drawing attacks, which means the interval between vendor patch and real-world exploitation has collapsed to roughly nothing. For anyone running internet-facing Cisco collaboration gear, that interval is the whole story: the patch shipped, adversaries moved, and defenders who treat a new fix as a next-sprint task are now operating inside the window where campaigns get built.</p>
<h4>Exposure Management Is Not a Mitigation Here</h4>
<p>Cisco Expressway exists to be reachable from the internet. That is its function — bridging collaboration and remote connectivity across organisational boundaries — and it is exactly what makes the platform attractive to attackers. Scanning infrastructure, credential-stuffing operations, and opportunistic exploitation all land hardest on assets that sit on public IP space, and an RCE on such a device sits directly in the path of remote-worker and partner traffic, with the potential to expose credentials, session tokens, and reach into the internal network.</p>
<p>It follows that knowing where your Expressway appliances are does not protect them. Inventory, exposure management, and asset visibility tell you a device is reachable by the internet; they do nothing to stop an exploit against it. <strong>Patching is the only real control on this class of flaw.</strong> Network segmentation and multi-factor authentication on surrounding gateways are worth having, but they limit blast radius after a compromise — they are not substitutes for applying the fix. For regional firms running Cisco collaboration and remote-access estate, edge appliances are consistently among the most heavily probed assets in any environment, and a critical flaw on one of them is not a routine patch ticket.</p>
<h4>A Practical Remediation Checklist</h4>
<p>Administrators responsible for Cisco Expressway deployments can work through the following sequence:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Inventory.</strong> Confirm whether Expressway appliances — or related Cisco collaboration edge components — are deployed anywhere in the estate, including remote offices and acquired subsidiaries.</li>
<li><strong>Confirm the fixed release.</strong> Check Cisco's advisory for the specific patched versions applicable to each deployed release train, and verify that the fix has actually been applied rather than merely scheduled.</li>
<li><strong>Prioritise internet-facing units.</strong> Appliances with public IP addresses or direct exposure to the internet should be patched first, ahead of internally segmented instances.</li>
<li><strong>Review logs.</strong> Examine appliance and authentication logs for unusual processes, configuration changes, or outbound connections that could indicate the flaw has already been leveraged.</li>
<li><strong>Harden adjacent infrastructure.</strong> Ensure multi-factor authentication and network segmentation are in place on surrounding remote-access and VPN gateways, limiting the blast radius of any edge compromise — while recognising that neither reduces the need to patch.</li>
</ol>
<h4>What Comes Next</h4>
<p>CISA's warning carries weight because it is backed by evidence of real-world exploitation rather than theoretical proof-of-concept code. Organisations that treat a vendor fix for a remotely exploitable flaw as a signal to accelerate — rather than queue — remediation generally fare better against bugs that have already crossed the threshold into criminal use.</p>
<p>BleepingComputer's report underlines a pattern IT teams in Hong Kong and across the region should plan around: the gap between a patch release and active exploitation keeps narrowing, and edge-facing infrastructure is where that gap closes first. The practical takeaway is straightforward — verify exposure, patch the internet-facing units first, and assume that unremediated edge devices will eventually be found.</p>

<h3>Cisco Expressway 新修補已出、攻擊者已在行動：CISA 指 RCE 漏洞正遭即時利用</h3>
<p>據 BleepingComputer 報導，美國網絡安全和基礎設施安全局（CISA）已通知聯邦民用機構，攻擊者正於野外利用 Cisco Expressway 中一個最近才修補的嚴重遠端執行碼（RCE）漏洞。</p>
<p>管理員閱讀這份安全通告時，應該改變看法的不是嚴重程度評級——而是時間表。一個剛剛才修補的漏洞已經有人發動攻擊，這意味著廠商發布補丁與現實世界中遭利用之間的間隔，已近乎歸零。對於任何運行面向互聯網的 Cisco 協作設備的人來說，這個間隔就是整件事的核心：補丁出爐、對手行動，而那些把新修補視為下個 sprint 才處理的防禦者，如今正身處攻擊行動建立的窗口之內。</p>
<h4>在此事上，暴露管理並非緩解措施</h4>
<p>Cisco Expressway 的存在目的就是讓外界能從互聯網連線——這正是它的功能，即跨組織邊界橋接協作與遠端連接——而這恰恰使該平台對攻擊者格外有吸引力。掃描基礎設施、credential stuffing（撞庫）行動及機會性利用，打擊最重的始終是坐擁公開 IP 空間的資產，而此類裝置上的 RCE 正好處於遠端員工及合作夥伴流量的必經路徑，有潛力洩露憑證、session token，並長驅直入內部網絡。</p>
<p>由此可見，知道你的 Expressway 裝置在哪，並不等於保護了它。資產清單、暴露管理與資產可見性只能告訴你某部裝置可被互聯網到達；它們無法阻止針對該裝置的利用。<strong>套用補丁才是此類漏洞唯一的真正控制手段。</strong>周邊閘道上的網絡分段（network segmentation）及多因素認證（multi-factor authentication，MFA）值得部署，但它們只能在系統被攻陷後限制爆炸半徑——並不能取代套用修補程式。對於運行 Cisco 協作及遠端訪問系統的地區企業而言，邊緣裝置一向是任何環境中被探測最多的資產之一，其中任何一部出現嚴重漏洞，都不是一張例行補丁工單可以處理的事。</p>
<h4>實用補救清單</h4>
<p>負責 Cisco Expressway 部署的管理員可按以下步驟逐一處理：</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>資產清單。</strong> 確認整個系統（包括遙距辦事處及已收購的附屬公司）是否在任何位置部署了 Expressway 裝置——或相關的 Cisco 協作邊緣組件。</li>
<li><strong>確認已修補版本。</strong> 查閱 Cisco 的安全通告，找出各已部署 release train 對應的具體已修補版本，並核實修補確實已經套用，而不只是排了期。</li>
<li><strong>優先處理面向互聯網的機組。</strong> 具有公開 IP 地址或直接暴露於互聯網的裝置，應先於內網分段的實例進行修補。</li>
<li><strong>檢視日誌。</strong> 檢查裝置及認證日誌，查找異常程序、設定變更或向外連接，以判斷漏洞是否已被利用。</li>
<li><strong>加固鄰近基礎設施。</strong> 確保周邊的遠端訪問及 VPN 閘道已啟用多因素認證及網絡分段，限制任何邊緣攻陷的爆炸半徑——同時要認識到，兩者都不能減少套用補丁的必要性。</li>
</ol>
<h4>接下來會怎樣</h4>
<p>CISA 的警告分量十足，因為它有現實世界中遭利用的證據背書，而非僅是理論上的 proof-of-concept 程式碼。把廠商針對可遠端利用漏洞的修補視為加快——而非排後——補救工作的信號的機構，在面對那些已經跨入犯罪用途門檻的漏洞時，一般表現更好。</p>
<p>BleepingComputer 的報導凸顯了一個香港以至整個地區 IT 團隊都應據此規劃的模式：補丁發布與活躍利用之間的差距不斷收窄，而邊緣面向的基礎設施正是這個差距最先閉合之處。實際結論很直接——核實暴露情況、先修補面向互聯網的機組，並假定未修補的邊緣設備終有一天會被人找到。</p>
