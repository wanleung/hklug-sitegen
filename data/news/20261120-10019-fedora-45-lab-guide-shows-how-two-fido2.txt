<h3>Fedora 45 Lab Guide Shows How Two FIDO2 Keys Keep You Signed In — and Unlocked</h3>
<p><em>Analysis: the first two sections summarise Fedora Magazine's walkthrough; the sections from "The trade-off nobody should skip" onward are our own commentary.</em></p>
<p>Fedora Magazine has published a walkthrough exploring how far a user can get on Fedora Linux 45 without typing an account password during normal use, relying instead on a physical FIDO2 security key.</p>
<p>The article is described as a follow-up to earlier work on PIN-and-touch authentication for encrypted storage, and it follows the author's first day working with Fedora Linux 45. Its premise is straightforward: see how long a typed account password can be avoided in daily use, with a hardware key handling authentication instead.</p>
<h4>Two keys, not one</h4>
<p>As Fedora Magazine lays it out, PIN-plus-touch authentication is applied at two points that most documentation tends to treat separately: unlocking encrypted storage, and signing into the desktop session. The walkthrough goes further by enrolling a second FIDO2 key alongside the first, so that one credential can be carried daily while the other is held in reserve — and the loss of either does not leave the user locked out of their own machine.</p>
<h4>Why the workflow framing matters</h4>
<p>The end-to-end shape of the exercise is what gives it reach beyond Fedora users. Plenty of documentation covers enrolling a FIDO2 key for a single service, and plenty more covers disk encryption on its own; fewer sources validate the whole chain — power on, unlock encrypted storage, log into the desktop, and move through authenticated steps without falling back to a typed password — in one pass.</p>
<h4>The trade-off nobody should skip</h4>
<p>In our view, the guide also surfaces a trade-off worth restating. A second FIDO2 key is, functionally, a bearer instrument (一紙不記名憑證): whoever holds it authenticates. That is the argument for carrying the backup key with the same care as the primary key — and for enrolling that backup key deliberately at setup time, while the first key is still being configured, rather than adding it weeks later as an afterthought once the original key is already in daily rotation and attention to recovery has faded.</p>
<h4>A note for phishing-resistant MFA programmes</h4>
<p>For IT teams outside the Fedora ecosystem, we think the exercise also reads as a useful template. Phishing-resistant MFA — FIDO2/WebAuthn in its hardware-backed form — appears with growing frequency in enterprise access policies, and pairing a second device mirrors the recovery planning such programmes need before rollout, not after the first token is lost.</p>

<h3>Fedora 45 實驗指南示範兩支 FIDO2 密鑰如何讓你持續登入——並保持解鎖狀態</h3>
<p><em>分析：首兩節摘述 Fedora Magazine 的教學文章；自「不容略過的取捨」起的各節則屬本網 own 評論。</em></p>
<p>Fedora Magazine 發布了一篇教學文章，探討在 Fedora Linux 45 上，用戶日常使用時可以走多遠而不輸入帳戶密碼，改以實體 FIDO2 安全密鑰負責認證。</p>
<p>文章被描述為早前研究加密儲存 PIN 加觸碰認證的延續，並記錄作者使用 Fedora Linux 45 首日的情況。其前提相當直接：看看日常使用中可以避免輸入帳戶密碼多久，由硬件密鑰代為處理認證。</p>
<h4>兩支密鑰，而非一支</h4>
<p>按照 Fedora Magazine 的闡述，PIN 加觸碰認證被應用於兩個多數技術文件傾向分開處理的環節：解鎖加密儲存，以及登入桌面 session。該教學更進一步，在第一支密鑰之外同時註冊第二支 FIDO2 密鑰，令一支 credential 可日常隨身攜帶，另一支留作備用——遺失任何一支都不會令用戶被自己的電腦拒諸門外。</p>
<h4>為何以工作流程作框架至關重要</h4>
<p>此練習端到端的完整形態，正是其影響力觸及 Fedora 用戶以外的原因。坊間有大量技術文件說明如何為單一服務註冊 FIDO2 密鑰，亦有更多文件單獨講解磁碟加密；能在單一流程中一次性驗證整條鏈路——開機、解鎖加密儲存、登入桌面，以及在各項已認證步驟之間切換而無需回退到輸入密碼——的來源則少得多。</p>
<h4>不容略過的取捨</h4>
<p>在我們看來，該指南亦帶出一個值得重申的取捨。第二支 FIDO2 密鑰在功能上就是一份不記名憑證（bearer instrument）：誰持有它便可完成認證。這正是以與主密鑰同等的謹慎態度攜帶備用密鑰的理據——也正因如此，備用密鑰應在設定階段刻意註冊，趁第一支密鑰尚在配置之時一併完成，而非數周後才隨意補上；屆時原有密鑰已投入日常輪換，對恢復方案的注意力亦已消退。</p>
<h4>給防釣魚 MFA 計劃的提示</h4>
<p>對於 Fedora 生態以外的 IT 團隊，我們認為此練習亦可視為一份有用範本。防釣魚 MFA——以硬件輔助形式實現的 FIDO2/WebAuthn——日益頻繁見於企業存取政策，而配對第二部設備正呼應此類計劃在推行前（而非首個 token 丟失後）所需的恢復規劃。</p>
