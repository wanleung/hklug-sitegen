Title: BADBOX 3.0 Found Pre-installed in Budget Android TV Boxes, Generating 40 Billion Ad Requests a Day, HUMAN Says

<p>BADBOX 3.0, the third version of the BADBOX botnet, is running on Android TV boxes as pre-installed software that owners never see and can't easily remove, according to research from HUMAN's Satori Threat Intelligence and Research team. The same operation is also firing off roughly 40 billion advertising requests every day — a volume researchers say makes most earlier ad-fraud efforts look small by comparison.</p>
<h4>Fraud hidden below the screen</h4>
<p>What distinguishes BADBOX 3.0 is where it sits. The malware is either written to the device before it reaches the owner or delivered through unofficial app stores and unverified firmware channels, rather than installed by the user. Because it lives beneath the layer most people ever look at, a box can behave perfectly normally on screen while quietly taking part in the botnet in the background. There is no obvious symptom for an owner to notice, and no conventional uninstall to try.</p>
<p>That is also what makes the campaign structurally hard to undo. With the malicious component at the firmware or pre-installed app level, the practical options narrow quickly to reflashing the firmware, replacing the device, or discarding it altogether. For hardware bought on price, the problem is compounded at the point of sale: buyers have little or no visibility into the software a device ships with, who maintains it, or whether security updates will ever arrive.</p>
<h4>Industrial-scale demand generation</h4>
<p>The 40-billion-a-day figure describes a change in how these infected boxes are being used. Earlier botnets of this kind were typically valued for the data they could steal or the traffic they could throw at a target. BADBOX 3.0's devices are instead being turned into machinery for manufacturing advertising demand — an automated pipeline that generates commercial impressions at a scale no human audience could produce.</p>

<h4>Relevance to Hong Kong deployments</h4>
<p>The attack's hardware bar is low: cheap streaming hardware with an unverified software stack is enough. The risk, in other words, does not depend on what a user clicks, but on what a device ships with. For IT professionals in Hong Kong, vetting vendors, firmware maintenance records, and update delivery before deploying this class of device is a defence that has to be in place long before an infection like this becomes possible.</p>

<p>據 HUMAN Satori 威脅情報與研究團隊的研究顯示，BADBOX 僵屍網絡第三版 BADBOX 3.0 現正以預裝軟件的形式運行於 Android TV 盒之中——用家既看不到它，也難以移除。同一行動每日發出約四百億次廣告請求，研究人員形容其規模令以往的廣告詐騙行動相形見絀。</p>
<h4>潛伏在畫面之下</h4>
<p>BADBOX 3.0 的關鍵在於它所處的位置。惡意程序在裝置送到用家手上之前已寫入系統，或經由非官方應用程式商店及未經認證的韌體渠道送抵裝置，而非由用家自行安裝。正因為它處於大多數人從不查看的層面，盒子在畫面上一切如常，卻在背景持續參與僵屍網絡。用家不會見到明顯症狀，也沒有常規的解除安裝方式可試。</p>
<p>這同時令該行動難以根除。當惡意元件位於韌體或預裝應用程式層級，實際可選的補救方法很快只剩下重新刷入韌體、更換裝置，或直接棄置。對以價格取勝的廉價硬件而言，問題在購買一刻已告加劇：買家對裝置出廠時附帶的軟件、由誰維護，以及日後是否會收到安全更新，幾乎或完全無從得知。</p>
<h4>工業級的廣告需求生成</h4>
<p>每日四百億次這個數字，描述的是這批受感染盒子用途上的轉變。過去同類僵屍網絡的價值主要來自竊取資料，或是向目標發動的流量；BADBOX 3.0 的裝置則被改造成製造廣告需求的機器——一條自動化 pipeline，以任何真人受眾都無法產生的規模生成商業曝光。</p>

<h4>對本港部署的相關性</h4>
<p>該行動對硬件的要求很低：一部廉價串流硬件，加上未經認證的軟件 stack，即是全部條件。換言之，風險不取決於用家點擊了甚麼，而在於裝置出廠時帶著什麼。對本港 IT 從業員而言，在部署這類裝置之前對供應商、韌體維護記錄及更新推送機制作出審核，是一道必須在感染發生之前就已到位的防線。</p>
