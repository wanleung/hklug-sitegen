Title: KDE Linux Turns Two: From Project Banana to First Alpha, Assessing the Desktop Community's Own Distro Experiment
Content:
<p>In recent years, immutable and atomic architectures have gained increasing traction across the Linux distribution landscape. Fedora offers Silverblue and Kinoite as its Atomic variants, openSUSE has introduced Kalpa, and Vanilla OS follows the same design philosophy. For end users in Hong Kong, the practical appeal is straightforward: a read-only root partition, updates delivered as whole-image snapshots, and the ability to roll back instantly when something goes wrong. For system administrators and developers who prioritise stability without wanting to hand-tune environments on a daily basis, this model is particularly attractive.</p>
<p>Now the desktop communities themselves are getting into the act. KDE Linux (linux.kde.org), the KDE Project's own distribution, has just marked its second year. According to KDE's own announcements, this represents the KDE community's second attempt at building a distribution. The project was first introduced under the codename "Project Banana" by Harald Sitter at the Akademy 2024 conference. Then, on 6 September 2025, Nate Graham announced the first alpha-quality build on his personal blog — a date that coincided with Akademy 2025.</p>
<p>KDE Linux follows a rolling-release model built on an Arch base with an immutable root filesystem, with the goal of achieving the tightest possible integration between the Plasma desktop and the underlying system. It is worth noting that KDE neon functions as a software delivery channel rather than a complete distribution; KDE Linux therefore represents the KDE community's first genuine, end-to-end distribution effort. A recent LWN.net report examining the project's two-year trajectory also noted that the division of labour between Graham and Sitter has been a key factor in keeping the project moving forward.</p>
<p>That said, alpha is alpha — the installer, hardware coverage, and update workflow are still undergoing shake-down and remain unsuitable for use as a daily driver. But for the KDE community itself, shipping an alpha build in the project's second year is itself a meaningful signal: upstream wants greater control over the desktop experience, the ability to fix problems immediately rather than waiting for downstream distributions to pick up the changes, and a tighter feedback loop. Over time, these improvements and lessons learned are expected to filter back into other Plasma-based distributions.</p>
<p>For Hong Kong's open-source community and IT departments, the more interesting question is not whether to install it right away, but how KDE's shift from "packaging the desktop for others" to "operating its own platform" will reshape the Plasma ecosystem. With the desktop's core community now backing the immutable model, the range of choices is only set to grow.</p>

<p>近年，不可變（immutable）及原子式（atomic）架構在 Linux 發行版界日漸普及。Fedora 以 Silverblue 及 Kinoite 作為其 Atomic 變體，openSUSE 推出 Kalpa，Vanilla OS 亦採用同一設計理念。對香港用家而言，其實際吸引力十分直接：根分區只讀、更新以整套映像快照形式推送，一旦出現問題可即時回退。對於重視穩定性、又不欲每日手動調校 environment 的系統管理員及開發人員而言，此模式尤其具吸引力。</p>
<p>如今桌面社群亦親自動手。KDE 計劃自家的發行版 KDE Linux（linux.kde.org）剛滿兩年。根據 KDE 自行公布，這代表 KDE 社群第二度試圖打造發行版。項目最初由 Harald Sitter 於 Akademy 2024 大會上以「Project Banana」代號對外介紹；其後於 2025 年 9 月 6 日，Nate Graham 在個人 blog 宣布首個 alpha-quality build——當日恰與 Akademy 2025 同期。</p>
<p>KDE Linux 採 rolling-release 模式，以 Arch 為基礎，root filesystem 不可變，目標是達成 Plasma 桌面與底層系統之間最緊密的整合。須留意 KDE neon 屬於軟件發放 channel，並非完整發行版；因此 KDE Linux 可謂 KDE 社群首個真正意義上端到端的發行版項目。LWN.net 近日一篇檢視該項目兩年發展軌跡的報導亦指出，Graham 與 Sitter 的分工，一直是推動項目持續向前的關鍵因素。</p>
<p>儘管如此，alpha 始終是 alpha——安裝器、硬件覆蓋面與更新流程仍處於 shake-down 階段，尚未適合作為日常主力機使用。但對 KDE 社群本身而言，在項目第二年便交付 alpha build，本身已是一個意味深長的訊號：上游希望對桌面體驗擁有更大控制權，能夠即時修復問題，無須等待下游發行版跟進採納改動，並縮短回饋循環。長遠而言，這些改進及累積經驗預期將逐步回流至其他以 Plasma 為基礎的發行版。</p>
<p>對香港開源社群及 IT 部門而言，更值得關注的問題並非是否即時安裝，而是 KDE 由「為他人打包桌面」轉向「自建平台」的轉變，將如何重塑 Plasma 生態。隨著桌面核心社群押注於 immutable 模式，可選擇的方案只會越來越多。</p>
