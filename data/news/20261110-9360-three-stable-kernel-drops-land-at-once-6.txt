<h3>Three Stable Kernel Drops Land at Once: 6.12.117 Carries the Largest Batch</h3>
<p>Greg Kroah-Hartman has released three stable kernels in a single batch: <strong>7.3.4</strong>, <strong>6.18.63</strong> and <strong>6.12.117</strong>. Users running any of those branches are encouraged to upgrade. The three releases arrive together, but they are not equal in size:</p>

<tr>

<td>More than 1,530</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>The 6.12.117 release is the largest of the three, carrying more than 1,530 patches — nearly double the 6.18.63 batch.</p>
<h4>What is in the batches</h4>
<p>The LWN report itself gives version numbers and patch counts only; the substance of each batch lives in the corresponding stable-branch release announcements for each version. Opening those announcements lets the changelog be read subsystem by subsystem.</p>
<p>For teams deciding whether to move this week or next, that changelog is the right place to start: scan it for subsystems and drivers that intersect with your deployed hosts — storage stacks, network drivers, virtualisation components, filesystem code — and treat those entries as the review queue. At 860 to 1,530 patches per release, line-by-line review is not a practical gate; everything outside your intersection rides along as-tested.</p>
<h4>Why the sizes differ</h4>
<p>The source report does not explain the disparity, but the pattern is consistent with how stable branches accumulate: a longer-lived branch collects fixes across a wider range of hardware and subsystems, while a recent branch has had fewer cycles to gather regressions. The 6.12 series is maintained as a long-term-support branch — it appears on <a href="https://www.kernel.org/releases.html">kernel.org's published list of supported releases</a> — and appears to have been in stable maintenance the longest of the three, which would naturally leave it carrying the broadest batch.</p>
<p>Long-term-support branches such as 6.12 are widely used to underpin enterprise and appliance deployments, which is one reason its batch tends to draw the most attention from operations teams — independent of where it sits in the release calendar.</p>
<h4>Suggested sequencing</h4>
<ol>
<li><strong>6.12.117 first for teams on that branch.</strong> It is the largest batch, so deferring it compounds the backlog for the next cycle.</li>
<li><strong>6.18.63 next.</strong> A smaller batch usually means lower integration friction and a faster validation pass.</li>
<li><strong>7.3.4 where relevant.</strong> Useful for teams tracking newer hardware or following mainline closely.</li>
</ol>
<p>In each case, stage the upgrade — validate against a representative subset of hosts before broad rollout, and keep the previous kernel available for rollback.</p>

<h3>同日發布三個穩定版核心：6.12.117 規模最大</h3>

<p>其中 6.12.117 規模最大，收錄逾 1,530 個修補，接近 6.18.63 批次的兩倍。</p>
<h4>這批修補實際包含什麼</h4>
<p>LWN 報導本身只提供版本號與修補數量，各批次的具體內容須到各版本對應的 stable 分支發布公告查閱，可逐個子系統閱讀 changelog。</p>
<p>對需要判斷應否即時升級的團隊而言，changelog 是正確起點：篩選與自身已部署主機相關的子系統與驅動（儲存、網絡、虛擬化、檔案系統），將其列為審核佇列。每次動輒 860 至 1,530 個修補，逐條審核並不現實；不在交集範圍內的項目，隨版本一併驗證上線即可。</p>
<h4>為何規模有別</h4>
<p>來源報導未有解釋差異，但此模式與 stable 分支的累積方式一致：存活時間越長的分支，涵蓋的硬件與子系統範圍越廣，累積的修正自然越多；較新的分支則回歸修補的週期較短。6.12 系列屬長期支援分支（列於 <a href="https://www.kernel.org/releases.html">kernel.org 已發布版本清單</a>），且看來是三者中維護時間最久的，批次最大並不意外。</p>
<p>6.12 這類 LTS 分支廣泛用於支撐企業與裝置類部署，這也是其批次往往最受營運團隊關注的原因之一——與它在發布日曆上的位置無關。</p>
<h4>建議次序</h4>
<ol>
<li><strong>用 6.12 分支的團隊優先升級 6.12.117</strong>——批次最大，延誤只會令下次累積更多。</li>
<li><strong>其次 6.18.63</strong>——批次較小，整合摩擦通常較低，驗證較快。</li>
<li><strong>7.3.4 按需要採用</strong>——適合追蹤新硬件或緊貼主線的團隊。</li>
</ol>
<p>三者均應分階段推行：先在具代表性的主機子集驗證，並保留舊核心以便回滾。</p>
