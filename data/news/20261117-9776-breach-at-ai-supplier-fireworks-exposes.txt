Title: Breach at AI supplier Fireworks exposes data of nearly 150,000 Uber drivers
Content:
<p>Uber is notifying close to 150,000 drivers that their personal information was stolen by attackers who broke into Fireworks, an AI infrastructure provider used by the ride-hailing company, as reported by BleepingComputer.</p>
<p>The intrusion at Fireworks took place in early October. According to BleepingComputer, the compromise occurred at the vendor rather than at Uber itself, but the stolen data included records belonging to Uber's driver population — pushing the notification count to nearly 150,000 people.</p>
<p><strong>Why the gig-worker angle matters</strong></p>
<p>The incident is a reminder that platform workers sit at a particular intersection of data-protection risk. Drivers for ride-hailing and delivery services hand over identifying details as a condition of earning a living; they do not choose their platform's vendors, and they are rarely the first to learn when something goes wrong upstream.</p>
<p>For readers in Hong Kong, the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance offers a useful general reference point for thinking about exposures of this kind, though this incident is not a local regulatory event and there is no indication of a Hong Kong nexus. The PDPO's core principles — that personal data be collected for a lawful purpose and not further used in a manner beyond that purpose, that reasonable security safeguards be applied, and that data processors be held to contractual security standards — are the same questions any jurisdiction would ask after a breach like this. It is also worth noting that Hong Kong's regime does not impose a statutory, time-bound breach-notification duty on data users, which means public disclosure of incidents affecting locally relevant platforms is often driven by overseas regulators or by the companies themselves rather than by a local legal trigger.</p>
<p><strong>The third-party dependency</strong></p>
<p>The more consequential story here is the supply chain. Fireworks is not a household name; it is an AI infrastructure provider — the kind of component supplier that sits several layers away from the end user. Yet a compromise at that layer was enough to expose the records of roughly 150,000 workers at one of the world's most recognisable technology platforms.</p>
<p>That pattern has become familiar in recent years: attackers increasingly find the shortest path to a large data set runs through a smaller vendor with a foothold inside a bigger company's environment. Each additional processor in the chain expands the attack surface without the data subject's knowledge or consent.</p>
<p>The published account, as reported by BleepingComputer, does not yet break down exactly which data fields were taken, nor how Fireworks was initially compromised. Those details matter for assessing the practical risk to affected drivers — a name and a payout figure carry different exposure potential than government identification numbers or bank details.</p>
<p><strong>What to watch</strong></p>
<p>Several questions remain open: whether the stolen data has appeared on leak forums, whether other Fireworks customers were affected, and whether any regulators have been alerted or have begun inquiries. Any of those developments would warrant a follow-up.</p>
<p>In the meantime, the incident is a straightforward case study for IT and security teams evaluating their own third-party estates. The question is no longer only whether your perimeter holds, but whether you can account for every downstream processor that touches your people's data — and what you would tell those people if one of them did not.</p>
<hr>
<p>據 BleepingComputer 報導，Uber 正通知近 15 萬名司機，他們的個人資料在攻擊者入侵 Fireworks 後被竊取；Fireworks 即 Uber 所使用的 AI 基礎設施供應商。</p>
<p>入侵發生於十月初。據 BleepingComputer 報導，這次事件的源頭在於供應商本身而非 Uber，但遭竊的資料包含屬於 Uber 司機群組的紀錄，使受通知人數接近 15 萬人。</p>
<p><strong>平台零工為何首當其衝</strong></p>
<p>是次事故提醒我們，平台工作者正處於資料保護風險的一個特殊交匯點。網約車及外賣服務的司機須交出身份資料方能賺取收入；他們無法選擇平台的供應商，上游一旦出事，往往也不是最早知情的一群。</p>
<p>對香港讀者而言，《個人資料（私隱）條例》（PDPO）可作為思考這類資料外洩的一般參考基準，惟此事件並非本地監管事件，亦無跡象顯示涉及香港關聯。PDPO 的核心原則——個人資料須為合法目的收集、不得超乎該目的進一步使用，須採取合理保安保障措施，以及須透過合約要求資料處理者達到保安標準——與任何司法管轄區在事故後會追問的問題一致。另須指出，香港制度並未就資料使用者設定具法定時限的資料外洩通報責任，換言之，涉及本地相關平台的事故，其公開披露往往由海外監管機構或企業本身推動，而非源於本地法律上的觸發點。</p>
<p><strong>第三方依賴</strong></p>
<p>較具深遠意義的其實是供應鏈。Fireworks 並非家喻戶曉的名字，而是一家 AI 基礎設施供應商——屬於那種與最終用戶相隔數層的元件供應商。然而，該層面的一次入侵，已足以暴露全球最具知名度的科技平台之一約 15 萬名人員的紀錄。</p>
<p>近數年這模式屢見不鮮：攻擊者愈來愈常發現，通往大型資料集的最短途徑，是經由一間在較大公司環境中已有立足點的小型供應商。鏈條上每多一個處理者，便在當事人不知情或未同意之下擴大攻擊面。</p>
<p>BleepingComputer 的報導目前尚未交代確切遭竊的資料欄位，亦未說明 Fireworks 最初如何被入侵。這些細節對評估受影響司機的實際風險至關重要——姓名與收入金額，和政府身份證明號碼或銀行帳戶資料，風險程度並不相同。</p>
<p><strong>留意動向</strong></p>
<p>數項問題仍有待釐清：遭竊資料有否出現在外洩論壇、其他 Fireworks 客戶是否受影響，以及是否有監管機構獲通知或已展開調查。任何一項發展都值得跟進。</p>
<p>同時，此事件對檢視自身第三方陣地的資訊科技及保安團隊而言，是個直接的案例。問題已不單是周邊防線能否守住，而是你能否掌握每一個接觸員工資料的下游處理者——以及若其中一個出事，你會如何向他們交代。</p>
