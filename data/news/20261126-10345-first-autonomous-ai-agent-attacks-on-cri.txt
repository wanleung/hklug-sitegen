Title: First autonomous AI agent attacks on critical infrastructure confirmed, Swiss and Italian CERTs say
Content:
<p>Switzerland's National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC) and Italy's Agenzia per la Cybersicurezza Nazionale (ACN) have issued a joint alert reporting what they describe as the first confirmed use of autonomous AI agents in offensive operations against critical infrastructure targets.</p>
<p>BleepingComputer reported the alert on 26 November 2026, relaying the two agencies' findings and their defensive guidance. According to that report, the activity under investigation targeted organizations in the critical infrastructure sector, with software agents carrying out multi-stage intrusion chains — reconnaissance, credential abuse and lateral movement — without a human operator driving each step.</p>
<p>The "first confirmed" wording is the agencies' own characterisation of their investigations rather than an independently verified finding, and that distinction should be kept intact: the claim is the substance of the alert, and it comes from two national security bodies tied to incidents they say they have confirmed.</p>
<h4>What the agencies say happened</h4>
<p>The distinguishing detail in the alert is autonomy, not AI assistance. AI-assisted phishing and model-generated malware have been documented for some time; what the agencies say they observed is software that plans and executes its own operations, iterating on results and changing path between runs. The practical impact lies in the tempo of the attack — a re-planned chain running at machine speed narrows the window between initial foothold and meaningful impact, and it produces activity that does not follow the familiar rhythm of scripted tooling, because the route can differ each run.</p>
<p>That combination is what defenders must design for: shorter clocks for detection, containment and human escalation, and intrusions whose shape is not stable enough to lock onto with static signatures.</p>
<h4>The mitigations, as a checklist</h4>
<p>Per the BleepingComputer report, the agencies' guidance leans on controls that are conventional but more urgent:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Harden identity:</strong> enforce multi-factor authentication, eliminate shared or standing privileged accounts, and apply least-privilege scoping to machine and service identities.</li>
<li><strong>Manage exposure:</strong> reduce the attack surface of internet-facing systems, patch known exploited vulnerabilities promptly, and segment networks so a single foothold cannot become a campaign.</li>
<li><strong>Detect behavior, not signatures:</strong> monitor for anomalous authentication patterns and unusual sequences of activity, since agent-driven intrusions may vary their tooling and their path.</li>
<li><strong>Preserve visibility:</strong> ensure logging covers identity systems, endpoints, and east-west traffic so compressed attack chains can still be reconstructed after the fact.</li>
<li><strong>Treat internal AI use as attack surface:</strong> organizations running their own agents should govern them like privileged third-party tools — scoped permissions, full logging, and human approval for consequential actions. An internal agent with broad credentials and no oversight is an attractive pivot point for anyone who compromises it.</li>
</ul>
<h4>Why it matters</h4>
<p>The advisory's value is institutional rather than novel: for two years, agentic-AI threat claims have largely come from vendor research blogs and proof-of-concept demonstrations, and defenders have learned to discount them. A joint warning from two national CERTs, tied to incidents they say they confirmed, carries different weight — and the controls being asked for are mostly the ones already on every security roadmap. What has changed is the case for doing them first.</p>

<hr>
<p>瑞士國家網絡安全中心（NCSC）與意大利國家網絡安全局（ACN）發出聯合通告，通報它們所指的首宗獲確認、由自主AI agent對關鍵基礎設施目標發動的攻擊。</p>
<p>BleepingComputer於2026年11月26日報道了這份通告，轉述兩間機構的調查結果與防禦指引。根據該報導，受調查的入侵活動針對關鍵基礎設施機構，由軟件agent自主執行多階段入侵鏈——偵察、憑證濫用及橫向移動——全程無需人類操作員逐步主導。</p>
<p>「首宗獲確認」的措辭是兩間機構對自身調查的定性，而非獨立核實的結論；這個區別應予保留：該說法正是通告的實質內容，並出自兩間宣稱已在相關事件中確認情況的國家安全機構。</p>
<h4>機構指稱的事件情況</h4>
<p>通告的關鍵在於「自主」，而非單純的AI協助。AI輔助釣魚及模型生成的惡意軟件早已有記錄；機構稱它們觀察到的，是能自行規劃和執行行動、根據結果迭代並在每次改變路徑的軟件。實際影響在於攻擊節奏——由機器速度重新規劃的入侵鏈，縮窄了從初步立足到造成實質影響的時間窗口；同時，這類活動不會遵循腳本化工具熟悉的規律，因為每次行經的路徑都可能不同。</p>
<p>防禦者必須針對這種組合設計應對方案：偵測、封堵以至交由人手跟進的時間都要縮短，而入侵活動的形態變化不定，難以用靜態特徵鎖定。</p>
<h4>緩解措施清單</h4>
<p>按BleepingComputer的報導，兩間機構的指引集中在一些屬基本、但更須優先落實的控制措施：</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>強化身份管理：</strong>強制實行多因素認證，消除共用或長期存在的特權帳戶，並對機器及服務身份套用最小權限設定。</li>
<li><strong>管理暴露面：</strong>縮小面向互聯網系統的攻擊面，及時修補已知被利用的漏洞，並分隔網絡，令單一立足點無法擴大成全面入侵。</li>
<li><strong>按行為而非特徵偵測：</strong>監察異常的認證模式及不尋常的活動序列，因為由agent驅動的入侵可能每次變換工具與路徑。</li>
<li><strong>保持可視度：</strong>確保日誌涵蓋身份系統、終端及東西向流量，使被壓縮的入侵鏈事後仍可重組還原。</li>
<li><strong>把內部AI使用視為攻擊面：</strong>自行部署agent的機構，應把它們視為獲特權的第三方工具來管治——限定權限範圍、完整記錄日誌，並要求重要操作經人工批准。一個擁有廣泛憑證而缺乏監察的內部agent，對入侵者而言是極具吸引力的跳板。</li>
</ul>
<h4>為何重要</h4>
<p>這份通告的價值在於機構層級，而非內容新穎：兩年來，關於agentic AI的威脅說法主要來自廠商研究網誌及概念驗證（proof-of-concept）示範，防禦者已習慣對此有所保留。由兩間國家CERT發出、並與它們宣稱已確認的事件掛鈎的聯合警告，份量截然不同——而當中要求的控制措施，大部分早已列在各人的安全路線圖上。改變的是優先落實它們的理由。</p>
