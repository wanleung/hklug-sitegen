Title: Italy Slaps Meta With €805M Fine Over Facebook's Default-On Android Gallery Access
Content:
<p>Italy's data protection authority has fined Meta €805 million over a Facebook for Android feature that pulled photos and videos from users' phone galleries and surfaced them as suggested posts, The Hacker News reported on 14 November.</p>
<p>The Garante's decision rests on two findings. The gallery-suggestion feature was enabled by default, so Italian users' images, videos and associated metadata — timestamps, geolocation tags and device identifiers — were read before any explicit consent had been obtained for that processing.</p>
<p>Meta has 60 days to contest the decision.</p>
<h4>What the Garante objected to</h4>
<p>The ruling targets how consent was obtained, not the existence of the feature. First, the feature shipped enabled by default, meaning gallery content was read before any user action indicated willingness. Second, whatever consent mechanism existed fell short of the GDPR standard: consent must be freely given, specific, informed and unambiguous — and it must be an affirmative opt-in, not something a user has to switch off.</p>
<p>That distinction is the crux of the case. A system permission prompt answers a narrow technical question — may this app read this directory? — and it does not, on its own, discharge the separate legal obligation to obtain consent for a defined processing purpose. The data here was accessed on an opt-out basis rather than an opt-in one, which is precisely what the Garante took issue with.</p>
<p>The metadata point matters too. Photos and videos rarely travel alone: the finding covers the contextual data attached to them, widening the footprint well beyond what a "suggested post" prompt implies to the average user.</p>
<h4>Why app teams should care</h4>
<p>For developers and compliance staff shipping mobile apps outside their home jurisdiction, the case is a useful stress test of permission UX. Italian enforcement has repeatedly focused on how consent is presented at the point of collection — default states, granularity and timing — rather than on whether a privacy policy exists somewhere in the settings tree. A feature that reads device storage at startup, before the user has engaged with it, is a conspicuous target regardless of what the app's terms say.</p>
<p>It also underlines that operating-system permissions and legal consent are parallel obligations, not interchangeable ones. Passing the Android runtime check does not mean the regulatory check has been passed.</p>
<p>For teams in Hong Kong and the wider region, the practical read is general rather than local: apps with overseas users remain exposed to foreign regulators' consent standards, and cross-border processing questions continue to sit alongside obligations under Hong Kong's own data protection regime. Any specific local requirements should be assessed against current guidance rather than assumed.</p>
<h4>A short checklist for app teams</h4>
<ul>
<li>Disable gallery-scanning or media-suggestion features until a user has affirmatively opted in — not merely failed to opt out.</li>
<li>Log when, how and in what wording consent was captured, and make those records retrievable.</li>
<li>Audit every startup touchpoint that touches device storage, and confirm nothing reads media before consent is recorded.</li>
<li>Ensure consent scope covers metadata — location, timestamps, device identifiers — not just the media files themselves.</li>
<li>Treat the OS permission dialog as a technical control, and document the separate legal basis for each processing purpose.</li>
</ul>
<p>Whether Meta contests the €805 million penalty within its 60-day window remains to be seen; the resolution could shape how similar default-on features are handled across the industry.</p>
<hr>
<p>據 The Hacker News 於 11 月 14 日報道，意大利資料保護機構因 Facebook for Android 的一項功能從用戶手機相簿中讀取照片及影片，並將其列為建議帖文顯示，對 Meta 處以 8.05 億歐元罰款。</p>
<p>Garante（意大利資料保護機構）的裁決基於兩項認定。首先，相簿建議功能屬預設啟用，因此意大利用戶的圖片、影片及相關 metadata —— 包括時間戳記、地理定位標籤及裝置識別符 —— 在未取得任何明確同意的情況下便已被讀取。</p>
<p>Meta 有 60 日期限就該裁決提出上訴。</p>
<h4>Garante 的異議所在</h4>
<p>該裁決針對的是同意的取得方式，而非功能本身是否存在。第一，該功能出廠時已預設啟用，意味著相簿內容在用戶作出任何表明意願的操作之前便已被讀取。第二，現有的同意機制未達 GDPR 標準：同意必須是自由作出、具體、知情且明確的 —— 而且必須是主動選擇加入（opt-in），而非需要用戶自行關閉的預設設定。</p>
<p>這正是本案的關鍵區別。系統權限提示只回答一個狹窄的技術問題 —— 此應用程式可否讀取該目錄？—— 這本身並不能履行就特定處理目的取得同意的獨立法律義務。本案中的資料是以退出機制（opt-out）而非加入機制（opt-in）方式存取，這正是 Garante 所針對的問題。</p>
<p>metadata 一點同樣重要。照片和影片很少單獨存在：該認定涵蓋附著於其上的 contextual data，使影響範圍遠超一般用戶對「建議帖文」提示的理解。</p>
<h4>應用開發團隊為何需要關注</h4>
<p>對於在其母國司法管轄區以外發佈流動應用程式的開發人員及合規人員而言，本案是檢視 permission UX 的有用壓力測試。意大利的執法一再聚焦於同意在收集環節如何呈現 —— 即預設狀態、粒度及時機 —— 而非私隱政策是否存在於設定選單的某處。一個在啟動時即讀取裝置儲存空間、甚至在用戶與其互動之前便已執行的功能，無論應用條款如何陳述，都會成為明顯的執法目標。</p>
<p>這亦凸顯作業系統權限與法律上的同意屬並行義務，而非可互相替代。通過 Android 的 runtime check，並不等於已通過監管機構的審查。</p>
<p>對香港及更廣泛地區的團隊而言，實務上的解讀屬一般性而非針對本地：擁有海外用戶的應用程式仍須面對外地監管機構的同意標準，跨境處理問題亦與香港自身資料保護制度下的義務並存。任何具體的本地要求，應根據現行指引加以評估，而非想當然。</p>
<h4>應用開發團隊簡要 checklist</h4>
<ul>
<li>在用戶主動選擇加入（opt-in）之前 —— 而非僅僅未選擇退出 —— 應停相簿掃描或媒體建議功能。</li>
<li>記錄同意於何時、以何種方式及何種措辭取得，並確保這些記錄可供查閱。</li>
<li>審查每一個涉及裝置儲存空間的啟動接觸點，確認在同意被記錄之前沒有任何媒體內容被讀取。</li>
<li>確保同意範圍涵蓋 metadata —— 位置、時間戳記、裝置識別符 —— 而不僅是媒體檔案本身。</li>
<li>對待 OS 權限對話框時應視其為技術層面的控制措施，並為每項處理目的另行記錄相應的法律依據。</li>
</ul>
<p>Meta 會否在 60 日期限內就 8.05 億歐元罰款提出上訴，尚待觀察；該結果可能會影響業界日後如何處理同類預設啟用的功能。</p>
