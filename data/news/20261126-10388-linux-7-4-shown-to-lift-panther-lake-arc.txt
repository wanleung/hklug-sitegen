Title: Linux 7.4 Shown to Lift Panther Lake Arc B390 Graphics Performance, But Gains Stay Modest
Content:
<p>```</p>
<p>Testing of Intel's Panther Lake platform on Linux points to a tiered path for extracting more performance from its integrated Arc B390 graphics: the Ubuntu 26.04 LTS install that ships on Dell's XPS 14 leaves headroom on the table, Ubuntu 26.10 recovers part of it, and the in-development Linux 7.4 kernel adds further gains concentrated in the Xe3 graphics stack — according to Phoronix, which laid out those findings in its coverage of the results.</p>
<p>The Dell XPS 14, powered by Intel's Core Ultra Series 3 "Panther Lake" processors, is offered with Ubuntu 26.04 LTS as one of Dell's supported operating system options. That makes 26.04 the natural baseline for the hardware as delivered. Phoronix's testing indicates, however, that simply moving to Ubuntu 26.10 yields measurable performance improvements, and that testing against the Linux 7.4 kernel — still in development — produces additional advantages, particularly for the Arc B390's integrated Xe3 graphics.</p>
<p>Readers looking for specific frame counts and throughput figures should consult Phoronix's full benchmark suite at the source link; the broad shape of the results is consistent across the tests: modest but real uplift, with the graphics stack rather than the CPU being where Panther Lake still has room to improve.</p>
<p><strong>Incremental, not transformative</strong></p>
<p>The size of the gains matters as much as their direction. These are incremental improvements, not a step change, and they fit a familiar pattern for Intel GPU hardware. A young architecture such as Xe3 typically depends on ongoing upstream driver work during its first year on market, with performance accruing steadily as the kernel and Mesa stacks mature. Panther Lake appears to be following the enablement timeline Intel's earlier graphics generations have followed, which means the "missing" performance on 26.04 LTS is less a flaw in the distribution than a reflection of where the platform sits in its lifecycle.</p>
<p><strong>A tiered upgrade decision</strong></p>
<p>For administrators deciding what to deploy on Panther Lake machines, the findings translate into three practical tiers:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Ubuntu 26.04 LTS</strong> remains the right choice for managed fleets where long-term support, predictable update cadence, and vendor backing matter more than throughput. The trade-off is a measurable graphics penalty during a platform's first year, when the shipped kernel lags the upstream work that benefits new silicon.</li>
<li><strong>Ubuntu 26.10</strong> is the middle path for workstations where performance matters but untested kernel versions do not — a reasonable default for individual power users and creative or engineering desktops.</li>
<li><strong>Linux 7.4</strong> is for testers and enthusiasts willing to run unreleased software to capture the additional Arc B390 uplift, accepting the stability and support caveats that come with a pre-release kernel.</li>
</ul>
<p>This framing is relevant beyond enthusiasts. Any organisation provisioning Panther Lake laptops has to weigh supportability against performance, and the benchmark results make the cost of choosing the safest option concrete rather than theoretical.</p>
<p><strong>Caveats before acting</strong></p>
<p>Linux 7.4 has not been released. Results measured against a development kernel are indicative, and performance, power behaviour, and stability may shift before the final release lands. Administrators should re-evaluate once 7.4 ships, and again when distributions backport relevant changes into their stable kernels — at which point some of today's advantage may arrive without leaving LTS at all.</p>
<p>The practical takeaway is that Panther Lake's Linux story is currently a graphics-driver story, and it will keep improving on its own schedule. Teams adopting the hardware now should pick their tier deliberately and plan a re-test rather than treat any single kernel version as a final answer.</p>

<p>```</p>
<p>針對 Intel Panther Lake 平台在 Linux 上的測試顯示，要從其整合式 Arc B390 圖形晶片榨取更多性能，存在一條分層遞進的路徑：Dell XPS 14 出廠預載的 Ubuntu 26.04 LTS 仍有性能餘量未被發揮，Ubuntu 26.10 可挽回部分損失，而開發中的 Linux 7.4 kernel 則進一步帶來額外提升，且增幅主要集中在 Xe3 圖形 stack——以上為 Phoronix 在其測試結果報導中列出的發現。</p>
<p>Dell XPS 14 採用 Intel Core Ultra Series 3「Panther Lake」處理器，Dell 提供 Ubuntu 26.04 LTS 作為其中一個受支援的操作系統選項。這使 26.04 成為該硬件出廠狀態下的自然基準。然而 Phoronix 的測試指出，單純升級至 Ubuntu 26.10 已可取得可量度的性能改善，而與 Linux 7.4 kernel——仍在開發中——進行測試，則產生額外優勢，尤其體現在 Arc B390 整合的 Xe3 圖形晶片上。</p>
<p>讀者如欲查閱具體 frame count 及 throughput 數據，可透過來源連結參考 Phoronix 的完整 benchmark 套件；各項測試的整體趨勢一致：溫和但確實的提升，而 Panther Lake 仍有改善空間之處在於圖形 stack，而非 CPU。</p>
<p><strong>屬漸進改善，非變革性突破</strong></p>
<p>提升幅度的大小與其方向同樣重要。這些是漸進式改進，而非跳躍式的質變，這與 Intel GPU 硬件的慣常模式相符。像 Xe3 這樣較新的架構，在面市首年通常依賴持續的上游驅動程式開發，隨著 kernel 與 Mesa stack 日趨成熟，性能穩步累積。Panther Lake 似乎正沿著 Intel 過往圖形世代所遵循的推動時間表發展，這意味著 26.04 LTS 上「缺失」的性能與其說是發行版的缺陷，不如說反映了該平台在生命周期中所處的階段。</p>
<p><strong>分層升級決策</strong></p>
<p>對於需要決定在 Panther Lake 機器上部署何種系統的管理員而言，上述發現可歸納為三個實際層級：</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Ubuntu 26.04 LTS</strong> 仍然是受管理機隊的正確選擇，尤其當長期支援、可預期的更新節奏及廠商支援比吞吐量更重要時。代價是平台首年會有可量度的圖形性能損失，因為出廠 kernel 落後於惠及新晶片的上游開發。</li>
<li><strong>Ubuntu 26.10</strong> 是中間路線，適合重視性能但不想使用未經測試 kernel 版本的工作站——對個別進階用家及創作或工程桌面而言，是合理的預設選擇。</li>
<li><strong>Linux 7.4</strong> 適合測試人員及發燒友，願意運行未發布的軟件以獲取 Arc B390 的額外提升，同時接受 pre-release kernel 所帶來的穩定性及支援方面的注意事項。</li>
</ul>
<p>此框架的意義不限於發燒友。任何部署 Panther Lake 筆記本的機構，都必須在可支援性與性能之間作出權衡，而 benchmark 結果使選擇最安全方案的代價變得具體，而非僅屬理論。</p>
<p><strong>行動前的注意事項</strong></p>
<p>Linux 7.4 尚未正式發布。以開發中 kernel 測得的結果僅供參考，性能、耗電表現及穩定性在最終版本推出前仍可能改變。管理員應在 7.4 正式發布後重新評估，亦應在各發行版將相關改動 backport 至其穩定 kernel 時再次檢視——到時部分今日的優勢可能無需離開 LTS 即可獲得。</p>
<p>實際結論是，Panther Lake 的 Linux 故事目前是一個圖形驅動程式故事，並會按自身節奏持續改善。現階段採用該硬件的團隊應審慎選擇所屬層級，並計劃日後重新測試，而非將任何單一 kernel 版本視為最終答案。</p>
