Title: US Homeland Security Moves to Strip DeepSeek From Federal Civilian Systems
Content:
<p><strong>US Homeland Security Moves to Strip DeepSeek From Federal Civilian Systems</strong></p>
<p>The US Department of Homeland Security has instructed federal civilian agencies to stop using DeepSeek products and remove them from government systems, according to a report by <a href="https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/dhs-bans-deepseek-products-across-us-federal-civilian-agencies/">BleepingComputer</a>. The scope described in the reporting covers both the Chinese AI vendor's applications and its footprint on government-owned devices and networks.</p>
<p>The available material comes with gaps readers should keep in view. No affected agencies are named, no guidance text from the directive is quoted, and no effective date is given. Neither DHS nor DeepSeek has commented directly in what has been published so far, and readers weighing the full scope of the order should consult the original BleepingComputer report.</p>
<p><strong>A question of visibility</strong></p>
<p>One point in the reporting is unambiguous: this is an instruction to remove software that is already in place, not a procurement rule discouraging future purchases. For any organisation, that distinction moves the operative capability away from policy drafting and towards software-asset inventory. Most cannot immediately answer a basic question — which DeepSeek components, if any, are running on our managed estate, and through which path did they arrive?</p>
<p>The scope definition reinforces the point. The material reviewed does not formally define what counts as a "DeepSeek product," leaving unclear whether the order reaches third-party applications embedding DeepSeek models. That fuzziness is itself the lesson: model access is frequently layered behind other products, so conventional package boundaries are hard to draw.</p>
<p>It also describes how these tools typically arrive. Model frameworks and hosted APIs rarely enter an enterprise through one sanctioned procurement channel — they come via developer sandboxes, proof-of-concept work, downloaded SDKs, or individual staff testing a public endpoint. An instruction to "remove from government systems" presumes someone can see the full footprint first.</p>
<p><strong>Why it reaches beyond Washington</strong></p>
<p>This is a US federal measure, and nothing in the source suggests comparable action by other governments or regulators. The order is a governance signal, not a template being acted on in Hong Kong or elsewhere.</p>
<p>The underlying questions, though, travel. Any move to pull a model off government infrastructure implies concern about what the model was being asked, what data reached it, and where that data sat afterwards. Teams running retrieval-augmented workflows against third-party endpoints should be able to answer the same questions about their own stack — not because of this vendor, but because the questions are vendor-neutral.</p>
<p><strong>A vendor-neutral diligence list</strong></p>
<p>For Hong Kong IT and security teams weighing Chinese or international LLM providers, the DHS decision reads best as a prompt rather than a directive:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Inventory:</strong> Which external model endpoints and SDKs are reachable from our managed devices today, and who approved them?</li>
<li><strong>Data flow:</strong> What categories of internal data are sent to inference endpoints, and does that match the data owner's consent?</li>
<li><strong>Contracts:</strong> Does our agreement cover sub-processors, telemetry, and data residency — and can we exit cleanly if policy changes?</li>
<li><strong>Fallback:</strong> If we had to remove a model from production within a week, what would break, and do we have an alternate inference route?</li>
<li><strong>Triggers:</strong> What events — vendor ownership changes, new government actions, security advisories — would force us to re-review the deployment?</li>
</ol>
<p>None of these presuppose a verdict on DeepSeek or on any other provider. They are the conditions under which a "yes" to any external model is defensible.</p>
<p>The takeaway is narrow and process-oriented: a major civilian security agency has decided one AI vendor's footprint on its systems is no longer acceptable and has moved to remove it. How quickly anyone else can make the same call — about this vendor or any other — depends on how well they can see their own environment.</p>

<p><strong>美國國土安全部下令民事機構移除 DeepSeek</strong></p>
<p>據 <a href="https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/dhs-bans-deepseek-products-across-us-federal-civilian-agencies/">BleepingComputer</a> 報道，美國國土安全部已指示聯邦民事機構停止使用 DeepSeek 產品，並將其從政府系統中移除。報道所述的範圍涵蓋這家中國人工智能供應商的應用程式，以及其在政府持有設備和網絡上的蹤跡。</p>
<p>現有資料存在若干空缺，讀者應予留意：沒有點名受影響的機構，沒有引用指令的指引原文，亦沒有公布生效日期。截至目前已發表的內容中，國土安全部與 DeepSeek 雙方均未直接置評；讀者如要評估命令的完整範圍，應查閱 BleepingComputer 的原始報道。</p>
<p><strong>可見性的問題</strong></p>
<p>報道中有一點十分明確：這是移除已安裝軟件的指令，而非阻止日後採購的採購規則。對任何機構而言，這一區別將實際操作的重點從政策擬定轉向軟件資產盤點。大多數機構無法立即回答一個基本問題——在我們管理的環境中，究竟有哪些 DeepSeek 組件（如有）正在運行？它們是透過什麼途徑進入的？</p>
<p>範圍界定進一步凸顯了這一點。已審閱的資料並未正式定義什麼算作「DeepSeek 產品」，因此命令是否涵蓋嵌入 DeepSeek 模型的第三方應用程式並不明確。這種模糊本身就是一項教訓：模型存取往往隱藏在其他產品背後，令傳統的套件邊界難以劃清。</p>
<p>報道亦描述了這些工具通常的進入方式。模型框架和 hosted API 很少透過單一經核准的採購渠道進入企業——它們往往經由開發者 sandbox、概念驗證（proof-of-concept）項目、下載的 SDK，或個別員工測試公開 endpoint 而流入。「從政府系統中移除」這一指令，預設了有人首先能夠看清完整的蹤跡。</p>
<p><strong>為何影響不限於華盛頓</strong></p>
<p>這是一項美國聯邦措施，來源資料並無暗示其他政府或監管機構有類似行動。該命令是一個治理信號，而非正在香港或其他地方落實的範本。</p>
<p>不過，其背後的問題具有跨地域的參考價值。任何將模型從政府基礎設施撤下的舉動，都意味著對以下事項的關注：模型曾被要求回答什麼、哪些數據曾送達模型、以及事後這些數據存放在哪裡。團隊如在第三方 endpoint 上運行 retrieval-augmented 工作流程，應能對自身技術棧回答同樣的問題——並非因為這家供應商，而是因為這些問題本身與供應商無關。</p>
<p><strong>一份與供應商無關的盡職審查清單</strong></p>
<p>對於正考慮中國或國際 LLM 供應商的香港 IT 及安全團隊而言，國土安全部的決定與其說是指令，不如說是提示：</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>盤點（Inventory）：</strong> 今日從我們管理的設備可連接哪些外部模型 endpoint 和 SDK？由誰批准？</li>
<li><strong>數據流向（Data flow）：</strong> 哪些類別的內部數據會傳送至 inference endpoint？這是否符合數據負責人的同意範圍？</li>
<li><strong>合約（Contracts）：</strong> 我們的合約是否涵蓋 sub-processor、telemetry 和數據駐留（data residency）？若政策改變，我們能否順利退出？</li>
<li><strong>後備方案（Fallback）：</strong> 如果我們須在一周內將某模型撤出 production，會有哪些環節失靈？我們是否有替代的 inference 路徑？</li>
<li><strong>觸發條件（Triggers）：</strong> 哪些事件——供應商股權變動、新的政府行動、安全通告——會迫使我們重新審視部署？</li>
</ol>
<p>這些問題都不預設對 DeepSeek 或任何其他供應商的結論。它們是對任何外部模型說「是」時得以站得住腳的條件。</p>
<p>總結而言，要點範圍很窄，且集中在流程：一家主要的民事安全機構已判定某人工智能供應商在其系統上的蹤跡不再可接受，並已採取行動將其移除。其他人要多快作出同樣判斷——無論針對這家還是其他供應商——取決於他們能在多大程度上看清自己的環境。</p>
