Title: SafePay ransomware passes 600 critical-infrastructure victims as CISA and FBI push phishing-resistant MFA
Content:
<hr />
<p>The SafePay ransomware operation has compromised more than 600 critical infrastructure organisations worldwide since 2024, according to a joint advisory from the US Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) and the FBI, as reported by BleepingComputer.</p>
<p>What the agencies are asking for is less a technical indictment of the group's tradecraft than a push on the fundamentals: phishing-resistant multi-factor authentication, tighter controls on remote access, and the working assumption that any exposed management interface will eventually be found.</p>
<h4>What the agencies are asking for</h4>
<p>The recommendations themselves are familiar; their framing is what stands out. CISA and the FBI are pressing organisations toward MFA that cannot be defeated by phishing or relay attacks — not SMS codes, and not push approvals that users can be talked into rubber-stamping — alongside limits on who can reach remote access tooling from the open internet.</p>
<p>Behind that sits the conventional hygiene checklist: offline or immutable backups that ransomware cannot reach, patching of internet-facing systems, network segmentation, and monitoring for abuse of legitimate administrative tooling. Defenders are being told to treat the presence of living-off-the-land activity as a signal in itself, rather than waiting for a ransom note.</p>
<p>Reports of SafePay's methods — purging volume shadow copies, enumerating directory services, interfering with endpoint security software — match prior reporting on the group, though the agencies' headline emphasis remains squarely on access hygiene rather than on novel technique.</p>
<h4>Why 600 matters</h4>
<p>Six hundred victims is not a record in ransomware terms, but the composition of the list is what draws attention. These are critical infrastructure organisations — the category of target where downtime propagates beyond the victim.</p>
<p>Two readings follow. The first is that this is an industrialised operation: a volume business that treats intrusion as a supply-chain problem rather than a bespoke one, hitting targets opportunistically wherever weak access control leaves a door open. The second is the uncomfortable one for the defence community — an operation leaning on widely available, comparatively unsophisticated tradecraft has still accumulated hundreds of critical-infrastructure victims. Novel attackers are not needed when basic access hygiene remains inconsistent.</p>
<p>The joint, cross-border framing of the advisory also signals how the agencies see the threat: as a transnational campaign that individual defenders cannot sit out. Notably, neither agency's public materials attribute the operation to a specific country or disclose whether payments have been made — an important caveat for anyone tempted to read more into the announcement than it says.</p>
<h4>The practical checklist</h4>
<p>For defenders, the convergence of advice is straightforward:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Phishing-resistant MFA</strong> on all remote access, administrative consoles, and email.</li>
<li><strong>Offline or immutable backups</strong>, tested for restoration, kept separate from production credentials.</li>
<li><strong>Perimeter patching</strong>, with special attention to VPN appliances, remote desktop gateways, and other internet-facing management surfaces.</li>
<li><strong>Detection of living-off-the-land activity</strong>, including legitimate tooling abused for lateral movement and shadow-copy deletion.</li>
</ul>
<p>None of these measures are new. That, arguably, is the point: an operation of this scale has been assembled against the defences organisations already know they should have in place.</p>
<h4>Relevance for Hong Kong practitioners</h4>
<p>For readers in Hong Kong, the story functions as an international reference point rather than a local regulatory development. Double-extortion ransomware — where data is exfiltrated before encryption and published if payment stalls — means a successful intrusion carries consequences beyond operational downtime: any exposure of personal data brings data-protection obligations into play under the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance, and organisations are well advised to review their breach-handling and notification procedures accordingly. The Privacy Commissioner for Personal Data's published materials on ransomware and data security remain the appropriate local reference for those procedures.</p>
<p>The lesson travels regardless of jurisdiction: the attacks described in the advisory succeed through gaps in access control, not through exotic vulnerabilities.</p>

<hr>
<p>據 BleepingComputer 報道，美國網絡安全和基礎設施安全局（CISA）與 FBI 聯合發布警告指，SafePay 勒索軟件行動自 2024 年以來已入侵全球超過 600 間關鍵基礎設施機構。</p>
<p>兩機構所要求的，與其說是對該組織技術手法的專業指摘，不如說是對基本功的敦促：採用抗釣魚的 multi-factor authentication（MFA）、加強遠程訪問管控，以及抱定一個工作前提——任何暴露的管理界面終有一天會被發現。</p>
<h4>兩機構的要求</h4>
<p>建議本身並不新鮮；令人注目的是其表述方式。CISA 與 FBI 正敦促機構採用無法被釣魚或 relay 攻擊擊破的 MFA——不是 SMS 驗證碼，也不是用戶容易被游說盲簽的 push 批准——同時限制誰可以從公開互聯網接觸遠程訪問工具。</p>
<p>背後是一份傳統的防護清單：勒索軟件無法觸及的離線或不可變備份（immutable backup）、對外系統的 patching、網絡分段（network segmentation），以及監控合法管理工具是否被濫用。防禦者被告知，應將 living-off-the-land 活動的出現本身視為一個信號，而非坐等勒索紙條出現。</p>
<p>關於 SafePay 手法的報告——清除 volume shadow copies、枚舉 directory services、干預 endpoint security 軟件——與過往對該組織的報道大致吻合，惟兩機構的重點強調仍集中在訪問衛生（access hygiene），而非新穎技術。</p>
<h4>為何 600 這個數字重要</h4>
<p>以勒索軟件而言，600 間受害機構並非紀錄，但名單的構成才是焦點所在。這些是關鍵基礎設施機構——屬於停擺影響會超越受害機構本身的目標類別。</p>
<p>由此有兩種解讀。其一，這是工業化的運作：一門規模化生意，將入侵視為供應鏈問題而非度身訂造的個案，伺機在任何防護薄弱、門戶大開之處出手。其二，對防禦界而言較為難堪——一個依靠廣泛可得、相對不複雜手法的行動，仍累積了數百間關鍵基礎設施受害者。當基本的訪問衛生仍不一致時，根本不需要新奇攻擊者。</p>
<p>警告的跨國聯合框架亦顯示兩機構如何理解此威脅：這是一場個別防禦者不能坐視其過去的跨國運動。值得注意的是，兩機構的公開材料均未將該行動歸因於特定國家，亦未披露贖金是否已支付——任何試圖從公告中讀取更多含義者，須留意此重要保留。</p>
<h4>實務清單</h4>
<p>對防禦者而言，各方建議的匯總相當直接：</p>
<ul>
<li>所有遠程訪問、管理控制台及電郵一律啟用 <strong>抗釣魚 MFA</strong>。</li>
<li><strong>離線或不可變備份</strong>，經還原測試，並與生產環境憑證分開保存。</li>
<li><strong>邊界 patching</strong>，特別留意 VPN 裝置、remote desktop gateway 及其他對外管理界面。</li>
<li><strong>偵測 living-off-the-land 活動</strong>，包括被濫用於 lateral movement 及刪除 shadow copy 的合法工具。</li>
</ul>
<p>以上措施沒有一項是新的。這正是重點所在：如此規模的行動，竟是針對機構早已明知應該具備的防禦而建立的。</p>
<h4>對香港從業者的啟示</h4>
<p>對香港讀者而言，此報導的意義在於提供國際參照，而非本地監管發展。雙重勒索（double extortion）式勒索軟件——在加密前先竊取資料，若未付款則公開——意味著成功入侵的後果不限於運作停擺：任何個人資料外洩均會觸發《個人資料（私隱）條例》下的資料保護責任，機構應相應檢討其資料洩露處理及通報程序。個人資料私隱專員公署就勒索軟件及數據安全發表的材料，仍是此類程序適當的本地參考。</p>
<p>此教訓不論司法管轄區皆然：警告中所述的攻擊之所以得逞，是源於訪問控制的漏洞，而非 exotic 漏洞。</p>
