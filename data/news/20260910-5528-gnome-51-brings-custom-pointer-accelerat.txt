Title: GNOME 51 Brings Custom Pointer Acceleration Profiles to Linux Desktop
Content:
<p>GNOME 51, set for release next week, will include a significant enhancement for precise input control: native support for custom pointer acceleration profiles. The feature, merged into the codebase as a last-minute addition, modifies both the Mutter compositor and the gsettings-desktop-schemas. This allows users to define their own acceleration curves for mice and trackpads.</p>
<p>This development integrates the feature at the system compositor level, ensuring consistent, session-wide behavior. By building it into Mutter and the desktop settings schema, the chosen acceleration profile applies everywhere, replacing the fragmented, application-specific workarounds many users have relied on. This approach aligns with GNOME's philosophy of providing robust defaults while expanding configurability for advanced needs.</p>
<h4>A System-Level Solution, Not a Workaround</h4>
<p>The core advancement lies in where the functionality is implemented. Because it operates at the compositor level, the pointer's feel remains uniform regardless of the active application. Users configure their preferred profile once, and it persists reliably across the entire desktop session.</p>
<p>This eliminates the need for power users to depend on command-line utilities or third-party input managers for fine-grained control. The configuration is now accessible directly through GNOME's native settings infrastructure, simplifying the user experience significantly.</p>
<h4>Serving Professional Workflows</h4>
<p>The impact is most pronounced in professional domains. Fields such as CAD, digital art, and video editing often demand precise, predictable pointer movement that generic defaults fail to provide. Linux gamers have also long sought more consistent and customizable mouse behavior.</p>
<p>For IT administrators managing fleets of workstations, the change removes a significant friction point. Teams deploying GNOME across multiple machines will no longer need to rely on custom scripts or per-user configuration hacks to ensure consistent input behavior.</p>
<h4>Parity with Peers</h4>
<p>The merge closes a well-known feature gap, bringing GNOME closer to parity with other desktop environments like KDE Plasma, which have offered comparable pointer customization for some time. Observers see this as a sign of healthy community momentum in refining fundamental user input.</p>
<p>The feature's last-minute inclusion suggests the implementation was judged stable and valuable enough for immediate integration. In GNOME's development process, such merges are not uncommon for patches deemed low-risk and high-value, especially when addressing a long-documented community need.</p>
<h4>What's Next for Users</h4>
<p>With the GNOME 51 stable release imminent, users will have the opportunity to test custom acceleration profiles. The primary question now is how quickly downstream distributions like Fedora, Ubuntu, and openSUSE will incorporate the change into their default packages, though early adoption is anticipated due to its low integration overhead.</p>
<p>This update underscores that even mature open-source desktop environments continue to evolve the core aspects of human-computer interaction, delivering tangible benefits to the professionals who rely on them daily.</p>

<h4>系統級解決方案，而非權宜之計</h4>
<p>核心進步在於功能實現的層級。由於其在合成器層級運作，指標的手感在任何活動應用程式下都保持一致。用戶只需配置一次偏好的配置，它便能在整個桌面操作會話中可靠地維持。</p>
<p>這消除了進階用戶對命令列工具或第三方輸入管理器的依賴，以進行細微控制。現時配置可直接透過 GNOME 的原生設定基礎架構存取，大幅簡化了用戶體驗。</p>
