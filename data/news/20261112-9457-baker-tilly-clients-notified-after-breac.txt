Title: Baker Tilly clients notified after breach at unnamed third-party provider

<h3>Baker Tilly clients notified after breach at unnamed third-party provider</h3>
<p>The Baker Tilly Advisory Group is writing to clients to warn that personal information about them — and, notably, about their dependents — was compromised in a data breach at a third-party service provider, according to BleepingComputer, which broke the story.</p>
<p>The details of the incident remain thin. The provider has not been identified, and neither the volume of records affected nor the categories of data involved have been disclosed. Baker Tilly did not respond to a request for comment, BleepingComputer reported. What is known is the more instructive part: the accounting and advisory network's own systems were not the point of entry. A supplier's environment was — and Baker Tilly is nonetheless the party sending the notices.</p>
<h4>The breach you didn't suffer is still your breach</h4>
<p>That asymmetry is the story. Outsourcing data processing shifts the operational burden of a compromise without shifting the reputational or fiduciary exposure. When a vendor is breached, the client firm is the name in the victim's inbox, the signatory on the notification letter, and the party that must answer for the due diligence that preceded the incident.</p>
<p>For professional services firms handling client records, the operative controls therefore sit upstream of the breach: what data is handed to vendors in the first place, what contractual notification timelines apply, whether audit rights exist, and whether the vendor's own security posture has been independently assessed rather than self-certified. A supplier's security posture is, in practice, your security posture.</p>
<h4>A blind spot hiding in plain sight: dependents</h4>
<p>One detail in the advisory deserves attention that incident summaries often bury: the notification covers clients' dependents. Children and spouses swept into household-level records — the kind of fields advisory engagements routinely collect, such as next-of-kin details or family addresses — are often absent from inventories that track only named account holders. They are simultaneously in scope for notification and the least likely to detect misuse of their identities, which may surface years later as account-opening fraud or synthetic-identity activity.</p>
<p>Firms building data inventories for vendor assessments would do well to ask a simple question of every processor: does the dataset include anyone other than the contracting client, and if so, how would they be reached in an incident?</p>
<h4>Networked firms, inconsistent disclosure</h4>
<p>Baker Tilly operates as a network of independently owned member firms, which introduces a subtler risk for anyone assessing the incident: disclosure practice can vary by jurisdiction. Clients in jurisdictions with stricter or better-understood breach expectations may receive fast, detailed letters, while others connected to the same event receive thinner or later notices — a consistency problem that network-level incident response plans are supposed to prevent but often don't.</p>
<p>For Hong Kong practitioners, the practical reference point is the Privacy Commissioner for Personal Data's advisory guidelines on personal data breach handling, which set out recommended assessment and notification practices rather than a statutory reporting mandate under the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. The guidance nonetheless signals the standard regulators expect: prompt assessment, documented decisions, and notification where there is a real risk of harm.</p>
<h4>The vendor-assessment checklist</h4>
<p>The transferable lesson from this incident is a short set of questions to put to any third-party processor:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Scope:</strong> Exactly what personal data — and whose — will the vendor hold?</li>
<li><strong>Terms:</strong> What contractual obligation, and what deadline, applies to breach notification?</li>
<li><strong>Visibility:</strong> Do we hold audit or attestation rights over the vendor's controls?</li>
<li><strong>Minimisation:</strong> Can we achieve the same outcome with less data, or none?</li>
<li><strong>Reach:</strong> If dependents or household members are in scope, can we contact them?</li>
<li><strong>Consistency:</strong> Will our clients in different jurisdictions receive the same disclosure?</li>
</ul>
<p>BleepingComputer did not report a timeline for the compromise, and it remains unclear whether the data has appeared on a leak site. Those unknowns — along with the provider's identity, the affected count, and the data categories — are the standing items worth watching for follow-up.</p>

<h3>第三方服務供應商遭入侵，Baker Tilly 通知客戶資料外洩</h3>
<p>據率先報道此事的 BleepingComputer 報道，Baker Tilly Advisory Group 正致函客戶，警告他們的個人資料——尤其值得注意的是，包括其受養人的資料——已在一宗第三方服務供應商的資料外洩事件中被洩露。</p>
<p>事件詳情至今仍十分有限。該供應商身分未獲披露，受影響的記錄數量及涉及的資料類別亦未公布。BleepingComputer 報道，Baker Tilly 未回覆置評請求。已知的事實反而更具啟示性：這家會計及顧問網絡本身的系統並非入侵點，遭入侵的是其中一家供應商的環境——然而發出通知的一方仍是 Baker Tilly。</p>
<h4>你未遭殃的外洩，仍然是你的外洩</h4>
<p>這種不對稱正是事件的重點。將資料處理外包，只會把營運負擔轉移到外洩事件上，卻不會轉移聲譽風險或受託責任。當供應商遭入侵，受害者的收件箱中出現的是客戶公司的名字，簽署通知信的是該公司，而事前盡職審查是否到位，也須由該公司交代。</p>
<p>對於處理客戶記錄的專業服務公司而言，關鍵控制措施其實在於外洩事件的上游：最初向供應商提供了甚麼資料、合約訂明的通知時限為何、是否存在審計權，以及供應商自身的安全狀況是經獨立評估，抑或僅屬自我認證。換言之，供應商的安全狀況，實際上就是你自己的安全狀況。</p>
<h4>隱而易見的盲點：受養人</h4>
<p>公告中有一個細節值得關注，而事故摘要往往將它掩沒：通知涵蓋客戶的受養人。被納入家庭層級記錄的子女及配偶——顧問項目經常收集這類欄位，例如緊急聯絡人資料或家庭地址——通常不會出現在只追蹤具名帳戶持有人的資料清單中。他們同時處於通知範圍之內，卻又是最不容易察覺自身身分被濫用的一群；有關濫用可能多年後才浮現，表現為開戶詐欺或合成身分（synthetic identity）活動。</p>
<p>企業在建立供應商評估用的資料清單時，最好向每個處理者提出一個簡單問題：資料集中除了締約客戶之外是否還包括其他人？如有，在發生事故時又如何聯絡他們？</p>
<h4>網絡式企業，披露標準不一</h4>
<p>Baker Tilly 以獨立持有的成員公司網絡形式運作，這為評估事件的人士引入了一重更微妙的風險：披露做法因司法管轄區而異。位於對外洩期望更嚴格或更清晰地區的客戶，可能迅速收到詳盡的信函；其他同屬一宗事件的客戶，卻可能收到內容較薄弱或較遲發出的通知——這是一致性問題，網絡層級的事故應對計劃理應防止，卻往往未能做到。</p>
<p>對香港從業員而言，實際參考基準是個人資料私隱專員公署就處理個人資料外洩發表的建議指引，當中列明建議的評估及通知做法，而《個人資料（私隱）條例》並無法定強制匯報規定。儘管如此，該指引仍顯示監管機構所期望的標準：盡快評估、記錄決策，以及在確有損害風險時發出通知。</p>
<h4>供應商評估清單</h4>
<p>從本事件可借鑒的教訓，是一組向任何第三方處理者提出的簡短問題：</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>範圍（Scope）：</strong> 供應商將持有哪些個人資料——屬於誰的資料？</li>
<li><strong>條款（Terms）：</strong> 資料外洩通知的合約責任及期限為何？</li>
<li><strong>可見度（Visibility）：</strong> 我們對供應商的控制措施是否持有審計權或認證權？</li>
<li><strong>最小化（Minimisation）：</strong> 能否以更少資料——甚至零資料——達到同樣效果？</li>
<li><strong>可聯絡性（Reach）：</strong> 如受養人或家庭成員在範圍之內，我們能否聯絡他們？</li>
<li><strong>一致性（Consistency）：</strong> 位於不同司法管轄區的客戶，會否收到相同的披露？</li>
</ul>
<p>BleepingComputer 未有報導入侵事件的時間表，資料是否已出現在洩露網站上亦仍屬未知。這些未知事項——連同供應商身分、受影響數量及資料類別——都是值得持續跟進的待觀察項目。</p>
