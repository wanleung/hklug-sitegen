Title: Firefox 161 Adds Per-Site VPN Switches and a Home for Classic Themes
Content:
<p>Mozilla has published release binaries for Firefox 161, the open-source browser's latest bi-weekly feature release. Headlining the update is per-site VPN toggling — a granular control that lets users route traffic through a virtual private network for individual domains rather than applying an all-or-nothing policy across an entire session. A dedicated Classic Themes section arrives alongside it as the release's second headline addition.</p>
<h4>Per-site VPN control</h4>
<p>Firefox 161 introduces the ability to switch VPN behaviour on or off for specific sites, according to Phoronix's coverage of the release. The practical effect is that users no longer have to choose between running everything through a tunnel or abandoning one entirely: a banking portal can be routed through the VPN while a local service that behaves poorly under one is left alone, all within the same browsing session.</p>
<p>The feature matters because it dissolves a trade-off that has long pushed people toward blunt instruments. Until now, anyone who wanted VPN coverage on a sensitive domain typically had to accept it everywhere — with the compatibility breakage and performance cost that entails — or reach for a third-party extension to manage exceptions. Firefox 161 moves that granularity into the browser itself. No extension is required, and no specific VPN provider integration is implied by the release; the source material names none.</p>
<h4>Classic Themes returns as a section</h4>
<p>The secondary addition is a Classic Themes section, giving the browser's long-requested styling options a dedicated home in the interface. Mozilla has not specified where these controls previously resided, so this is best read as a consolidation and discoverability change rather than a wholly new capability: users gain a predictable place to find and manage classic theme options, rather than a new class of feature.</p>
<p>Cosmetically, that sounds minor. In practice, personalisation controls in a browser tend to get used — and browsers that bury them tend to get complaints. The point of a dedicated section is that the options are findable without a search engine.</p>
<h4>Availability and release context</h4>
<p>Firefox 161 binaries are downloadable now through Mozilla's usual channels, with Linux distribution packages typically following a short while later — a lag that is worth remembering before assuming a system package manager has picked the release up. As with any point release, teams managing browser fleets across managed desktops will want to validate their extensions and internal web applications before rolling 161 out broadly; profiles and settings carry over as they do with standard updates.</p>
<p>This is a routine feature release in Mozilla's bi-weekly cadence rather than a milestone version. That low-risk profile makes it a convenient moment for IT teams to test the new VPN controls against their own web estates.</p>
<h4>Why it matters</h4>
<p>Two trends converge in Firefox 161. The first is the migration of privacy features out of the extension ecosystem and into the browser core — the direction Mozilla has been travelling for years, and one that competitors are following. The second is the expectation, now firmly established among users, that privacy and personalisation controls should be fine-grained rather than global. Per-site VPN switching is the substantive version of that expectation; the Classic Themes section is the cosmetic one.</p>
<p>For anyone tracking release trains or managing deployments, the headline is straightforward: a low-risk update that hands users (and administrators) more granular control without requiring third-party tooling. For the wider open-source community, it is a signal that the browser, not the add-on, is increasingly where privacy configuration is expected to live.</p>
<p>Details in this article are drawn from Phoronix's coverage of the release; teams relying on the new controls for production use should confirm behaviour against Mozilla's official release notes before deployment.</p>
<hr>
<p>Mozilla 已發布 Firefox 161 的正式版本檔案，這是這款開源瀏覽器最新的雙週功能更新。本次更新的主打功能是逐個網站的 VPN 切換控制——一項細緻的控制選項，讓使用者可以就個別網域將流量透過虛擬私人網絡（VPN）傳送，而非在整個瀏覽時段一刀切地套用同一政策。與此同時，一個專屬的「經典主題」（Classic Themes）版面亦同步登場，成為本次更新的第二項焦點新增。</p>
<h4>逐個網站的 VPN 控制</h4>
<p>根據 Phoronix 對是次發布的報導，Firefox 161 引入了針對特定網站開關 VPN 功能的能力。實際效果是使用者從此不必在「所有流量一律經由隧道」與「完全放棄 VPN」之間二擇其一：銀行網站可以透過 VPN 接入，而本地服務若在 VPN 下表現不佳則可維持原狀，全部都在同一個瀏覽時段內完成。</p>
<p>此功能之所以重要，在於它化解了一直以來令人們退而求其次、採用粗糙方案的取捨。在此之前，任何想為敏感網域加上 VPN 保護的使用者，通常都要麼在所有網站一律啟用——隨之而來是兼容性問題與性能代價——要麼借助第三方擴充功能來管理例外情況。Firefox 161 將這種細緻度直接帶入瀏覽器本身。此功能無需安裝任何擴充功能，該版本亦沒有指明與任何特定 VPN 供應商的整合；原始材料中並無提及任何供應商。</p>
<h4>經典主題回歸成為獨立版面</h4>
<p>第二項新增是一個「經典主題」版面，為瀏覽器這項長期被要求的樣式選項在介面中提供專屬位置。Mozilla 並未指明這些控制項過往存放在何處，因此較準確的理解是：這是一次整合與提升可發現性的改動，而非全新的功能——使用者獲得一個可預期的位置去尋找及管理經典主題選項，而非新增一整類功能。</p>
<p>從表面上看，這似乎微不足道。但在實踐中，瀏覽器的個人化控制項往往是會被使用的——而將這些控制項埋藏起來的瀏覽器則往往招致投訴。設立專屬版面的意義，在於這些選項無需借助搜索引擎便可找到。</p>
<h4>可用性與發布背景</h4>
<p>Firefox 161 的版本檔案現在已可透過 Mozilla 的慣常渠道下載，Linux 發行版的軟件包通常會稍後才跟進——在假設系統套件管理器已經收錄該版本之前，這段時間差值得留意。與任何點版本更新一樣，負責管理受控桌面環境瀏覽器機群的團隊，在廣泛部署 161 之前應先驗證其擴充功能及內部網絡應用程式；設定檔與設定會如標準更新一樣得以沿用。</p>
<p>這是 Mozilla 雙週發布節奏下的例行功能更新，並非里程碑版本。這項低風險的特性，令它成為 IT 團隊測試新 VPN 控制項是否配合自身網絡資產運作的合適時機。</p>
<h4>為何重要</h4>
<p>Firefox 161 集合了兩大趨勢。其一是私隱功能由擴充功能生態遷移至瀏覽器核心——這是 Mozilla 多年來的方向，亦是競爭對手正在跟隨的道路。其二是使用者已經牢固確立的期望：私隱與個人化控制應當細緻分級，而非全局一刀切。逐個網站的 VPN 切換是這一期望的實質體現；經典主題版面則是其外在體現。</p>
<p>對於追蹤發布列車或管理部署的人員而言，重點很明確：一次低風險更新，讓使用者（以及管理員）獲得更細緻的控制，而無需依賴第三方工具。對更廣泛的開源社群而言，這是一個信號：私隱配置預期的所在地正越來越是瀏覽器本身，而非附加元件。</p>
