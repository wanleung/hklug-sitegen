Title: Linus Torvalds Calls Linux 7.4-rc4 'Huge' as AI-Generated Reports Strain Reviewers
Content:
<p>Linus Torvalds did not mince words about the fourth release candidate of Linux 7.4, tagging it with a familiar refrain: "It's huge. What a surprise."</p>
<p>The remark, published alongside the rc4 tag and reported by Phoronix, caps another active week — a test cycle running well ahead of where a mid-stream release candidate normally sits. Phoronix frames AI/LLM-generated bug reports and patches as a driver of the cycle's activity, though the rc4 announcement itself does not tie that traffic to rc4's specific size.</p>
<h5>Why the volume matters</h5>
<p>Release candidates exist to shrink a kernel cycle, not to enlarge it. The first rc absorbs the bulk of a merge window; each subsequent candidate should progressively narrow the diffstat as regressions are found and fixed. An rc4 that still draws a "huge" characterization from the maintainer who tags it suggests the cycle is not converging in the usual way.</p>
<p>The more substantive angle is not the novelty of machine-written contributions — AI-generated submissions have by now moved from curiosity to routine in this cycle — but the triage workload they place on subsystem owners. Torvalds has repeatedly framed reviewer bandwidth, not code volume, as the binding constraint in kernel development: every patch or bug report, regardless of origin, still has to be read, evaluated, and either merged or rejected by a human with the expertise and the hours to do so. That work falls hardest during a period when the cycle is supposed to be narrowing toward a final release.</p>
<h5>What this means for testers</h5>
<p>For administrators and infrastructure teams, the guidance around release candidates is unchanged: keep rc kernels off production systems, test only on disposable hardware, pin real workloads to a stable or vendor-supported kernel, and file regressions during the rc window while feedback can still shape the final release. For Hong Kong IT professionals running Linux fleets, that last point matters most — the release-candidate phase is a community QA mechanism, not a preview channel for early adopters in the data centre.</p>
<h5>The bigger picture</h5>
<p>Torvalds has previously warned that low-quality or careless patches — whether a human or a model produced them — consume reviewer time the kernel community does not have to spare, and that review capacity remains the resource the 7.4 cycle is really spending. Whether rc5 brings the cycle back toward its usual cadence will be the next test.</p>

<hr>
<p>Linus Torvalds 對 Linux 7.4 的第四個 release candidate 毫不掩飾，以一句熟悉的口吻為其標記：「它很巨大。真叫人驚訝。」</p>
<p>這番評語與 rc4 標記一同公布，並由 Phoronix 報導，為這個測試周期又一個活躍的星期畫上句號——整個周期的進度遠超一般中期 release candidate 所處的水平。Phoronix 將 AI／LLM 生成的 bug 報告及 patch 視為推動周期活躍的因素，不過 rc4 的公告本身並未將這股流量與 rc4 的具體規模直接掛鉤。</p>
<h5>為何報告數量至關重要</h5>
<p>Release candidate 的存在是為了縮短核心開發周期，而非令其延長。第一個 rc 承接了 merge window 的大部分內容；其後每一個 candidate 應隨著回歸問題（regression）被發現及修正，逐步收窄 diffstat。一個由負責標記的維護者仍形容為「巨大」的 rc4，意味著這個周期並未按常規收斂。</p>
<p>更具實質意義的角度，並不在於機器撰寫貢獻的新穎程度——AI 生成的提交在這個周期早已由新奇事物變成家常便飯——而在於它們為 subsystem 負責人帶來的分類（triage）工作量。Torvalds 多次指出，核心開發的關鍵限制因素是審核者的時間，而非程式碼數量：每一個 patch 或 bug 報告，不論來源為何，仍然必須由具備相關專業知識及時間的人類逐一閱讀、評估，然後合併或拒絕。在周期理應收窄、邁向最終發布的階段，這項工作負擔最為沉重。</p>
<h5>對測試人員的影響</h5>
<p>對管理員及基礎設施團隊而言，圍繞 release candidate 的指引維持不變：切勿在生產系統上使用 rc 核心，只在可棄置的硬件上測試，將實際工作負載固定於穩定版本或廠商支援的核心，並趁 rc 窗口期內提交回歸問題，使反饋仍能影響最終發布。對管理 Linux 機羣的香港 IT 專業人士而言，最後一點尤為重要——release candidate 階段是社群的 QA 機制，而非供數據中心 early adopter 使用的預覽渠道。</p>
<h5>大局展望</h5>
<p>Torvalds 此前曾警告，低質素或粗疏的 patch——不論由人還是模型產生——都會消耗核心社群本已捉襟見肘的審核時間，而審核能力正是 7.4 周期真正耗用的資源。rc5 能否令周期回復正常節奏，將是下一個考驗。</p>
