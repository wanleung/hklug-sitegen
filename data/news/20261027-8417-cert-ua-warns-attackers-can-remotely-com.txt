<h3>CERT-UA Warns Attackers Can Remotely Commandeer Dahua Recorders via Easy4IP</h3>
<p>Ukraine's national computer emergency response team, CERT-UA, has warned that attackers are gaining unauthorised <strong>remote</strong> access to Dahua IP cameras and digital video recorders through the vendor's Easy4IP cloud service — allowing them to watch live video feeds and modify device settings.</p>
<p>In an alert dated 23 October 2026, as reported by Security Affairs, CERT-UA said intruders were taking over video surveillance systems made by the Chinese manufacturer. No specific models, firmware versions, CVE identifiers, or indicators of compromise were disclosed in the public alert, and a follow-up advisory or vendor statement has not yet appeared.</p>
<h4>Why Hong Kong readers should pay attention</h4>
<p><strong>Editor's note:</strong> The section below provides local market context based on general observations about Dahua's presence in Hong Kong; it is not based on confirmed incidents.</p>
<p>The implications extend well beyond Ukraine. Dahua hardware is among the most widely deployed CCTV brands in Hong Kong, appearing across residential estates, retail outlets, warehouses, and schools. This article is not a report of locally confirmed incidents — no Hong Kong deployment has been publicly tied to this campaign — but a large installed base of the same vendor's devices means the underlying weakness is a shared, market-wide concern for the territory's IT and facilities teams.</p>
<h4>The cloud relay, not the perimeter, is the weak point</h4>
<p>The mechanism matters more than the branding. Easy4IP is a cloud relay capability that makes devices reachable from the internet through Dahua's own infrastructure. While the feature is switched on, an attacker does not need to breach an on-site firewall, punch a hole in a VPN, or defeat any network-level control — the vendor's cloud broker handles the connectivity. Traditional perimeter defences are effectively bypassed by design.</p>
<p>CERT-UA identified two distinct outcomes of a successful compromise. The first is privacy and situational-awareness failure: attackers can view live streams from cameras that were supposed to be private. The second is an integrity and pivot risk: settings can be rewritten, credentials potentially overwritten, and the device becomes a foothold inside networks that are frequently flat and lightly monitored — CCTV segments are typically installed by contractors and rarely receive the same scrutiny as corporate or point-of-sale systems.</p>
<p>There is also a privacy dimension worth flagging for local organisations handling footage of identifiable people. Video feeds showing recognisable individuals are commonly treated as personal data, and under Hong Kong's Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance, organisations are generally expected to take practicable steps to secure the personal data they hold. Cameras that a third party can reach from anywhere in the world complicate that obligation considerably.</p>
<h4>What defenders should do now</h4>
<p>CERT-UA's alert does not provide a patch path, so the response should start with what defenders can check themselves today:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Map the exposure.</strong> Enumerate every Dahua device, record firmware levels, check whether Easy4IP or P2P features are enabled, and test whether devices are publicly reachable.</li>
<li><strong>Disable cloud relays you don't need.</strong> Prefer self-controlled connectivity — a VPN or reverse tunnel you operate — over vendor-brokered access.</li>
<li><strong>Segment.</strong> Place CCTV on its own VLAN, isolated from office, POS, and building-management networks.</li>
<li><strong>Harden credentials.</strong> Enforce unique, non-default passwords per device.</li>
<li><strong>Audit cloud accounts.</strong> Review vendor and third-party cloud logins tied to surveillance equipment, revoke access that is no longer needed, and enable multi-factor authentication where the platform supports it.</li>
<li><strong>Patch, and plan for end-of-life.</strong> Devices that cannot be updated should be scheduled for replacement rather than left in service.</li>
<li><strong>Monitor and restrict.</strong> Watch logs for anomalous sessions and unexpected outbound connections; disable UPnP, Telnet, and FTP, and constrain egress.</li>
</ol>
<p>The investigation remains ongoing. Security Affairs published its report of the CERT-UA alert at the source URL above; no public response from Dahua was available at the time of writing. We will update this article if CERT-UA issues a follow-up advisory with affected models or remediation guidance, or if the company comments.</p>

<h3>CERT-UA 警告攻擊者可透過 Easy4IP 遠端操控大華錄像設備</h3>
<p>烏克蘭國家電腦事故應變中心 CERT-UA 發出警告，指攻擊者正透過大華（Dahua）的 Easy4IP 雲端服務，未經授權<strong>遠端存取</strong>大華 IP 攝影機及數碼錄像機，從而觀看即時視像畫面及修改設備設定。</p>
<p>據 Security Affairs 報道，CERT-UA 於 2026 年 10 月 23 日發出的通告中表示，入侵者正在接管這家中國製造商生產的視像監控系統。該公開通告並未披露具體型號、韌體版本、CVE 編號或入侵指標（IoC），後續補充通告或廠商聲明亦尚未出現。</p>
<h4>為何香港讀者應留意</h4>
<p><strong>編者按：</strong> 以下章節提供本地市場背景，依據的是關於大華在香港市場佔有情況的一般觀察，並非基於已確認事故的報道。</p>
<p>有關漏洞的影響範圍遠超烏克蘭。大華硬件是香港部署最廣泛的 CCTV 品牌之一，遍佈住宅屋苑、零售店舖、倉庫及學校。本文並非本地已確認事故的報告——目前沒有任何香港部署被公開與該攻擊行動關聯——但同一廠商設備的龐大裝機量意味著，這項底層缺陷是本港 IT 及設施團隊共同面對的市場級問題。</p>
<h4>雲端 relay 而非網絡邊界才是弱點</h4>
<p>關鍵在於運作機制，而非品牌標籤。Easy4IP 是一項雲端 relay 功能，讓設備可透過大華自身的基礎設施從互聯網連接。只要該功能處於啟用狀態，攻擊者便毋須突破現場防火牆、在 VPN 開洞或繞過任何網絡層級防護——連接由廠商的雲端 broker 處理，傳統邊界防禦實際上在設計上已被架空。</p>
<p>CERT-UA 確認了入侵成功後的兩種不同後果。第一是私隱及態勢感知失效：攻擊者可以觀看本應保密的攝影機即時串流。第二是完整性及橫向移動風險：設備設定可被改寫、憑證可能被覆蓋，而設備本身則成為進入網絡的立足點——這些網絡往往結構扁平、監控鬆散，CCTV 網段通常由承辦商安裝，極少受到與企業系統或銷售點（POS）系統同等的審視。</p>
<p>對於處理可辨識人物影像的本地機構，另有私隱層面的問題值得指出。顯示可辨認人士的視像畫面通常被視為個人資料，根據香港《個人資料（私隱）條例》，機構一般須採取切實可行的措施保障其持有的個人資料。當第三方可從世界任何角落連接攝影機，這項義務便大大複雜化。</p>
<h4>防禦方現時應採取的措施</h4>
<p>CERT-UA 的通告並未提供修補方案，因此防禦方應先著手處理今天便可自行核查的事項：</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>摸清暴露面。</strong> 列舉每一台大華設備，記錄韌體版本，檢查 Easy4IP 或 P2P 功能是否已啟用，並測試設備是否可從公共網絡連接。</li>
<li><strong>停用不需要的雲端 relay。</strong> 優先採用自主控制的連接方式——自行運作的 VPN 或反向隧道——而非依賴廠商 broker 的存取。</li>
<li><strong>網絡分段。</strong> 將 CCTV 置於專屬 VLAN，與辦公、POS 及樓宇管理網絡隔離。</li>
<li><strong>強化憑證。</strong> 每台設備強制使用獨一無二、非預設的密碼。</li>
<li><strong>審核雲端帳戶。</strong> 檢視與監控設備相關的廠商及第三方雲端登入，撤銷不再需要的存取權限，並在平台支援的情況下啟用多重身份驗證（multi-factor authentication）。</li>
<li><strong>修補並規劃退役。</strong> 無法更新的設備應安排更換，不應繼續運作。</li>
<li><strong>監控及限制。</strong> 留意日誌中的異常會話及非預期的向外連接；停用 UPnP、Telnet 及 FTP，並限制出站流量（egress）。</li>
</ol>
