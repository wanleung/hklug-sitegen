Title: 64% of Leaked Secrets From 2022 Still Work — Teams Stall on What a Revoke Will Break
Content:
<p>More than 64% of valid secrets first discovered in 2022 were still working in January 2026, according to GitGuardian's <em>State of Secrets Sprawl 2026</em>, as reported by The Hacker News on 25 November 2026. In other words, credentials exposed four years ago remained usable well into this year — not because teams failed to find them, but because no one could say with confidence what revoking them would break.</p>
<p>That finding reframes the secrets-management conversation. Detection tooling has improved to the point where leaked API keys, tokens and passwords surface quickly; what stalls is remediation. A rotation touches production systems, third-party integrations and long-forgotten scripts, and teams wary of outages simply defer the decision. The result is a growing backlog of live credentials sitting in public channels, waiting for an owner who never quite arrives.</p>
<p>The second key figure in the report sharpens the prescription: triaging findings on validity alone misses 46% of critical secrets. In other words, asking only "does this credential still work?" filters out nearly half of the secrets that actually matter. The missing dimensions are impact — what a credential can access, and whether it leaked into publicly reachable channels rather than a private log.</p>
<p><strong>From observation to operation</strong></p>
<p>For Hong Kong DevOps and security teams, the practical translation is an impact-weighted rotation workflow rather than a validity-driven queue. A workable six-step checklist looks like this:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Inventory</strong> — collect every detected secret and its repository or channel of origin.</li>
<li><strong>Classify blast radius</strong> — record what the credential can reach: production data, cloud infrastructure, payment or customer-facing services.</li>
<li><strong>Check exposure</strong> — distinguish public leaks from internal-only occurrences; public exposure escalates priority immediately.</li>
<li><strong>Rotate → revoke → verify</strong> — issue the replacement credential, kill the old one, and confirm traffic has moved, in that order, so no window of unnecessary downtime opens.</li>
<li><strong>Assign ownership</strong> — every secret gets a named accountable team, not a shared inbox.</li>
<li><strong>Report remediation, not discovery</strong> — measure "secrets remediated" as the headline metric rather than "secrets found," since found-but-live credentials are the actual risk.</li>
</ol>
<p>Step 4 is where most stalls occur, and it is also where a staged rotation — deploying the new credential before revoking the old — reduces the blast-radius anxiety that causes teams to defer.</p>
<p><strong>Disclosure</strong></p>
<p>GitGuardian is a commercial vendor of secrets-detection software and has a direct commercial interest in the report's findings; the analysis above is based on figures as relayed by The Hacker News and should be read with that context in mind.</p>
<p><strong>Why this matters</strong></p>
<p>The report's underlying conclusion is that credential exposure has become an ownership problem, not a detection problem. Tools will keep finding secrets faster than organisations can retire them unless teams change what they optimise for: rank findings by exposure and blast radius, name an owner for each, and hold rotation queues to an impact-based service level.</p>
<p>For IT professionals generally, the uncomfortable takeaway is that a clean detection dashboard can coexist with years-old credentials still authenticating in production. The metric that matters is not how many secrets the scanner surfaced — it is how many stopped working.</p>
<p>According to The Hacker News, the full report and its updated prioritisation guidance were published alongside the findings.</p>
<hr>
<p>根據 GitGuardian 的《State of Secrets Sprawl 2026》，並經 The Hacker News 於 2026 年 11 月 25 日報道，首次於 2022 年發現、至今仍然有效的 secrets 中，超過 64% 至 2026 年 1 月仍在運作。換言之，四年前已遭曝光的憑證，至今仍然可用 — 這並非因為團隊找不到它們，而是因為沒有人能有把握地斷言：撤銷這些憑證究竟會弄壞甚麼。</p>
<p>這項發現重新框定了 secrets 管理的討論。偵測工具已有長足改進，外洩的 API key、token 和密碼很快就會浮現；真正停滯的是補救（remediation）環節。一次 rotation 會牽動生產系統、第三方整合以及早被遺忘的腳本，團隊憂慮引致服務中斷，索性將決定一再推遲。結果是越來越多仍然生效的憑證堆積在公開渠道，等待一個永遠沒有真正出現的負責人。</p>
<p>報告中第二個關鍵數字進一步釐清了處方：單憑有效性來分類處理 findings，會漏掉 46% 的 critical secrets。換言之，只問「這個憑證還能用嗎？」，便會過濾掉近半真正重要的 secrets。欠缺的兩個維度是影響範圍 — 一項憑證可以存取甚麼，以及它是流入了公眾可達的渠道，還是僅存在於私人 log 之內。</p>
<p><strong>由觀察走向操作</strong></p>
<p>對香港的 DevOps 和安全團隊而言，實際可行的做法，是以影響範圍為權重的 rotation 工作流程，而非由有效性主導的處理隊列。一份可行的六步清單如下：</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>盤點（Inventory）</strong> — 收集每一個已偵測到的 secret，以及其所屬 repository 或渠道來源。</li>
<li><strong>評定 blast radius</strong> — 記錄該憑證可觸及的範圍：生產數據、cloud infrastructure、支付或面向客戶的服務。</li>
<li><strong>檢查曝光程度（Check exposure）</strong> — 區分公開外洩與僅在內部出現的情況；一旦屬公開曝光，其優先次序應即時提升。</li>
<li><strong>輪換 → 撤銷 → 驗證（Rotate → revoke → verify）</strong> — 按次序簽發替換憑證、終止舊憑證，並確認流量已經轉移，以免出現不必要的停機窗口。</li>
<li><strong>指派負責人（Assign ownership）</strong> — 每個 secret 都須有具名的負責團隊，而非一個共享 inbox。</li>
<li><strong>匯報補救成效，而非偵測數字（Report remediation, not discovery）</strong> — 以「已補救的 secrets 數目」作為首要指標，而非「已發現的 secrets 數目」，因為已發現但仍在生效的憑證才是真正的風險。</li>
</ol>
<p>第 4 步是大多數工作停滯之處，亦正正是在這裡，分階段 rotation — 先部署新憑證、再撤銷舊憑證 — 能夠減輕那種令團隊推遲決定的 blast radius 焦慮。</p>
<p><strong>利益披露</strong></p>
<p>GitGuardian 是商業 secrets 偵測軟件供應商，與該報告的發現有直接商業利益；以上分析乃基於 The Hacker News 所轉述的數據，讀者宜在此背景下閱讀。</p>
<p><strong>為何重要</strong></p>
<p>報告的根本結論是：憑證曝光已演變成一個權責歸屬問題，而非偵測問題。除非團隊改變優化的目標，否則工具找到 secrets 的速度，將持續快於企業淘汰它們的速度。團隊應：按曝光程度與 blast radius 為 findings 排序、為每一項指明負責人，並以影響範圍為基礎的服務水平來約束 rotation 隊列。</p>
<p>對一般 IT 專業人士而言，最令人不安的啟示是：一個乾淨整潔的偵測 dashboard，可以與多年來仍然在生產環境中進行驗證的憑證並存。真正關鍵的指標，並非 scanner 找出了多少 secrets — 而是有多少已經停止運作。</p>
